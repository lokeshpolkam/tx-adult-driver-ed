Statutory Passing Rules, Sight Distance & The 200-Foot Safety Buffer

In Texas, paint striping on roadways carries statutory legal force. Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.055, passing on a two-lane two-way highway is strictly prohibited unless the passing side is marked with a broken yellow line and the left lane is clearly visible and free of oncoming traffic for a distance sufficient to complete the pass without interfering with safe operation.

rule CARDINAL TEXAS PASSING MANDATES
  • • The 200-Foot Mandatory Return Buffer (§ 545.055(b)): An operator passing to the left MUST return to an authorized lane before coming within 200 feet of any approaching vehicle. Failure to complete the pass before this boundary is a serious moving violation.
  • • Double Solid Yellow Lines (§ 545.055): Strictly prohibits passing from both directions. Turning left across a double solid yellow into a private driveway, alley, or business entrance is permitted, but traveling or overtaking in the oncoming lane is strictly illegal.
  • • Passing Prohibitions (§ 545.056): An operator may NEVER drive to the left side of the roadway when approaching within 100 feet of an intersection, railroad grade crossing, bridge, viaduct, or tunnel, or when cresting a hill or rounding a curve where view is obstructed.
  • • Center Two-Way Left-Turn Lane (TWLTL - § 545.060): Bounded by solid outer yellow and dashed inner yellow lines. Strictly for deceleration and left turns. Illegal to use for passing or traveling straight for more than 200 feet.

ZONE 01 // BROKEN YELLOW LINE TTC § 545.055

Passing Permitted With Clear Forward Sight Distance

Passing is lawful from either direction when the broken yellow line is on your side. The passing driver must verify the opposing lane is clearly visible and free of oncoming traffic for a sufficient distance (minimum 200 ft clearance before returning to right lane prior to any oncoming vehicle). Speeding while passing is illegal under Texas law.

Stop Bars, Continental Crosswalks & Pedestrian Yield Protocol

Unlike longitudinal lines that separate moving streams of vehicles, transverse markings cut across travel lanes to dictate exact legal stop points and pedestrian sanctuary zones.

STOP LINE (STOP BAR)

Solid white line (12-24 in wide). Mandatory stop boundary. Front bumper must not cross line before stopping.

CONTINENTAL CROSSWALK

High-visibility ladder bars. Under TTC § 552.003, driver must yield to pedestrians within half of roadway or approaching closely.

YIELD SHARK TEETH (TMUTCD § 3B.16): A row of solid white isosceles triangles pointing directly toward approaching vehicles indicates the point at which an approaching vehicle must yield to cross traffic or pedestrians, common at roundabouts and mid-block crossings.

NODE 01 // STOP LINE LEGAL BOUNDARY TTC § 544.007(d)

Mandatory Complete Stop Before The Transverse Stop Line

Texas law mandates that an operator facing a steady red signal or stop sign shall stop before entering the crosswalk on the near side of the intersection, or if there is no crosswalk, before entering the intersection. When a transverse stop line is marked, the front bumper must stop completely before crossing or overhanging the line.
