HSC § 365.012 • TEXAS LITTER ABATEMENT ACT CRIMINAL STATUTE

(a) A person commits an offense if the person dumps or permits the dumping of litter or other solid waste at a place that is not an approved solid waste site, including on or within 300 feet of a public highway, right-of-way, or other public property.
(b) Discarding items from a moving or stopped vehicle—such as cigarette butts, beverage cans, plastic bottles, fast-food bags, or allowing loose trash to blow from an open truck bed—constitutes an explicit criminal offense.
(c) Mandatory judicial sanctions include court-ordered community service, requiring between 8 and 60 hours of highway roadside litter collection in high-visibility safety vests under county supervision. Subsequent convictions elevate the offense to the next higher misdemeanor or felony tier.

• 300-Foot Jurisdictional Envelope
The statutory boundary extends 300 feet beyond the edge of pavement, encompassing drainage ditches, highway rights-of-way, access roads, bridge embankments, and scenic view corridors across Texas.
TTC CH. 725 • SECURING LOADS ON VEHICLES MANDATORY CONTAINMENT

§ 725.001 – § 725.021: An operator transporting loose gravel, sand, dirt, construction timber, tree branches, household appliances, furniture, or scrap metal on public roadways must physically secure the cargo to prevent any material from blowing, spilling, dropping, or leaking onto the pavement.
Tarping & Tie-Down Mandates: Open-bed pickup trucks and utility trailers carrying loose items must be covered with a closely fitted tarpaulin or heavy-duty canvas cover securely tied down on all four sides. Bulky items (mattresses, ladders, toolboxes) must be anchored with rated commercial ratchet tie-down straps capable of withstanding dynamic aerodynamic drag forces at 70 MPH ($F_{drag} = C_d A \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2$).

Class C Misdemeanor for Discarding Litter Under 5 Pounds

Under Texas Health and Safety Code § 365.012(d), discarding waste weighing 5 pounds or less (or having a volume of 5 gallons or less) onto a public highway or right-of-way constitutes an illegal dumping offense punishable as a Class C Misdemeanor. Penalties include a fine up to $500 and mandatory community service (8 to 60 hours) performing roadside cleanup. This applies to thrown cigarette butts, beverage cans, fast food containers, and blowing bed trash.

The Physics of Highway Debris Impact: KE = ½ m v²

Highway speed dramatically amplifies collision forces due to velocity squaring. A driver cruising at 70 MPH covers 102.7 feet per second ($v = \text{MPH} \times 1.467$). Striking a stationary 10-lb road debris fragment (such as a shredded commercial tire casing or 2x4 timber) delivers:

Kinetic Energy Mathematical Proof:
m = 10 lbs ÷ 32.174 ft/s² = 0.3108 slugs
v = 70 MPH × 1.4667 = 102.67 ft/s
KE = ½ × 0.3108 × (102.67)² = 1,638 FT-LBS OF ENERGY

This delivers an instantaneous impact shock equivalent to dropping a 1-ton concrete block from nearly a foot in height directly onto a vehicle windshield. However, violent swerving to avoid this impact subjects the chassis to lateral tire trip forces that exceed roll stability thresholds ($a_{lat} \ge 0.65g$), causing vehicles to flip uncontrollably into ditches or oncoming interstate lanes.

"Don't Mess with Texas" & 1-888-TEX-LITTER Reporting

Launched by TxDOT in 1986, "Don't Mess with Texas" is the official roadway preservation initiative that has reduced roadside litter by over 70%. When motorists observe trash being discarded from a moving vehicle, they can report the violation via the toll-free hotline 1-888-TEX-LITTER (1-888-839-5488) or at dontmesswithtexas.org. Submitting the license plate number, date, time, and highway location triggers an official TxDOT warning letter, educational packets, and vehicle litter bags mailed to the registered vehicle owner.
