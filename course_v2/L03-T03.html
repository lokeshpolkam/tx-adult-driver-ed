Multi-Scenario Right-of-Way & Intersection Collision Matrix

Conflict Arbitration, Priority Hierarchies & Texas Move Over Corridor

Under Texas jurisprudence, right-of-way is not an absolute personal right to be taken—it is a statutory duty to yield granted or surrendered under specific geometric rules. Texas Transportation Code Chapter 545, Subchapter D establishes an unyielding priority hierarchy designed to prevent lateral and T-bone collisions at intersections, ramps, and roadway transitions.

Whether resolving sequential First-In, First-Out (FIFO) arrivals or simultaneous right-hand yields at 4-way stops, navigating uncontrolled T-junctions, or clearing high-speed corridors under the Texas Move Over or Slow Down Law (TTC § 545.157), failure to yield carries severe civil liability, license surcharges, and criminal sanctions escalating to State Jail Felonies.

verified_user Fundamental Texas Legal Doctrine (TTC § 545.151):

Right-of-way must always be yielded when necessary to avoid a collision. Even when statute grants precedence, an operator is never legally entitled to force their way into a hazardous conflict zone. In Texas, failure to yield resulting in bodily injury mandates a fine of $500 to $2,000, escalating to $1,000 to $4,000 for serious bodily injury.

SEQUENTIAL STOP // FIFO TTC § 545.151

First-to-Stop Has Right-of-Way

At an intersection controlled by stop signs on all approaches, the driver of the vehicle that stops FIRST has the right-of-way to proceed first. If Car A arrived and completely stopped before Car B, Car A proceeds while Car B yields.

Texas Right-of-Way Priority Matrix

Authoritative conflict resolution rules under Texas Transportation Code Chapter 545:

Controlled-Access Frontage Road TTC § 545.154

An operator on an access or frontage road of a controlled-access highway MUST YIELD right-of-way to a vehicle entering or about to enter the access road from the highway, or leaving or about to leave the access road to enter the highway. Freeway ingress/egress maintains absolute priority over frontage road cruise traffic.

Simultaneous Center Lane Merge TTC § 545.061

On a roadway divided into three or more lanes moving in the same direction, a vehicle entering a lane of traffic from the RIGHT shall yield the right-of-way to a vehicle entering the same lane of traffic from the LEFT. (The driver on the left has legal precedence into the center lane).

Paved vs. Unpaved Roadways TTC § 545.151(e)

An operator on an unpaved road approaching the intersection of a paved road shall yield the right-of-way to all vehicles operating on the paved road. Surface engineering establishes automatic legal priority.

Stopped School Bus Flashing Red TTC § 545.066

An operator approaching a stopped school bus with alternating flashing red lamps must stop before reaching the bus from either direction. Divided Highway Exception: You need not stop if operating on a separate roadway of a divided highway separated by a physical median, barrier, or unpaved dividing space (a center two-way left-turn lane does NOT qualify). Fine: $500 to $1,250 (1st offense); up to State Jail Felony for serious injury.

PRIMARY MANDATE // VACATE LANE TTC § 545.157(a)(1)

Vacate the Lane Closest to the Stopped Responder

On a roadway with two or more lanes traveling in the same direction, Texas law requires drivers approaching a stopped emergency vehicle with flashing lights to vacate the lane closest to the emergency vehicle, provided it is safe to do so.

Protected Responder Scope (TTC § 545.157(a)):

The Move Over or Slow Down law applies to stationary: (1) Police, Fire, and EMS emergency vehicles; (2) Tow trucks displaying emergency overhead warning lights; (3) Texas Department of Transportation (TxDOT) maintenance and construction vehicles; (4) Utility service provider vehicles; and (5) Municipal solid waste and recycling collection trucks.
