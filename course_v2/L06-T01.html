Dual-Track Intoxication Jurisprudence & BAC Measurement Radar

Under Texas law, chemical intoxication is adjudicated along two distinct, independent legal tracks. A motorist is guilty of Driving While Intoxicated (DWI) either through clinical impairment—lacking normal use of mental or physical faculties by introduction of alcohol, drugs, or combinations thereof—or through chemical liability per se with an alcohol concentration of 0.08 or higher. For minors under 21, Texas enforces Zero Tolerance: any detectable amount triggers criminal DUI charges under Texas Alcoholic Beverage Code § 106.041.

TPC § 49.01(2) • DUAL-PRONG DEFINITION CORE DOCTRINE

“Intoxicated” means:
(A) Clinical / Behavioral Prong: Not having the normal use of mental or physical faculties by reason of the introduction of alcohol, a controlled substance, a drug, a dangerous drug, a combination of two or more of those substances, or any other substance into the body; or
(B) Per Se Chemical Prong: Having an alcohol concentration of 0.08 or more.

• Evidentiary Independence of the Two Prongs
The two prongs are disjunctive. High tolerance does not protect a driver with 0.08+ BAC. Conversely, an operator testing at 0.05 BAC who swerves across lane lines and fails SFST field sobriety is fully convicted of DWI under Prong (A).
TPC § 49.04 • DWI CRIMINAL PENALTY MATRIX SANCTIONS
  • Standard 1st Offense (Class B Misdemeanor): Minimum 72 hours confinement (6 days if open container present under TPC § 49.031), fine up to $2,000, and 90 to 365 days license suspension.
  • Aggravated BAC ≥ 0.15 (Class A Misdemeanor • TPC § 49.04(d)): Confinement up to 1 year, fine up to $4,000, and mandatory Ignition Interlock Device (IID).
  • DWI with Child Passenger < 15 Years (State Jail Felony • TPC § 49.045): 180 days to 2 years state jail, fine up to $10,000.
  • Intoxication Assault (Third-Degree Felony • TPC § 49.07): Serious bodily injury; 2 to 10 years prison.
  • Intoxication Manslaughter (Second-Degree Felony • TPC § 49.08): Causing death; 2 to 20 years prison, fine up to $10,000.
  • Prescription Defense Ban (TPC § 49.10): Holding a lawful prescription is NO defense to DWI.
TABC § 106.041 • ZERO TOLERANCE FOR MINORS AGE < 21 DUI

Texas Alcoholic Beverage Code § 106.041 establishes the offense of Driving Under the Influence of Alcohol by a Minor (DUI): Any minor operating a vehicle or watercraft with any detectable amount of alcohol commits a Class C misdemeanor (fine ≤ $500, 20-40h community service, alcohol awareness class, 60-day ALR suspension). Under subsection (g), if BAC is ≥ 0.08 or faculties are lost, charge escalates to adult DWI under TPC § 49.04.

Chemical Concentration Ceiling: 0.08 BAC

In Texas, having an alcohol concentration of 0.08 or more is intoxication per se. The State is not required to prove clinical stumbling, slurred speech, or mental confusion. The chemical number alone satisfies proof of intoxication beyond a reasonable doubt for Class B Misdemeanor DWI.

ZERO-ORDER LIVER ELIMINATION FORMULA β60 CONSTANT
BAC(t) = BAC₀ − (β₆₀ × t)

Where BAC₀ is peak alcohol concentration, β₆₀ is the fixed hepatic zero-order elimination rate (averaging 0.015 g/dL per hour), and t is elapsed time in hours. Because liver alcohol dehydrogenase enzyme pathways saturate rapidly, alcohol does NOT clear faster when concentration is high.

• Time to drop 0.120% peak to legal 0.080%:
t = (0.120 − 0.080) / 0.015 = 2.67 Hours (2h 40m)
• Time to reach full 0.000% sobriety:
t = 0.120 / 0.015 = 8.00 Hours
