Vehicle Passenger Area vs. Lawful Storage Geometry

STATUTORY FRAMEWORK • TPC § 49.031 & § 49.04(c)

TEXAS LAW
1. Statutory Definition: What Is an "Open Container"? TPC § 49.031(a)(1)

Texas law defines an "Open Container" as any bottle, can, flask, or receptacle that contains any amount of alcoholic beverage and that:

  • Is open or has a broken manufacturer's seal;
  • Has been previously opened and re-closed (e.g., re-corked wine);
  • Has its original contents partially removed; OR
  • Contains even a few remaining drops or residual dregs.

No "resealing" exceptions exist once factory seals are severed, even if closed with decorative stoppers or original screw caps.

2. The "Passenger Area" Perimeter & Exclusions TPC § 49.031(a)(2)

Under Texas Penal Code § 49.031(a)(2), the "passenger area" means the space designed for seating operator and passengers. This includes seats, consoles, floorboards, and door pockets.

PROHIBITED CABIN: Driver & passenger seats, cup holders, floorboards, consoles.
STATUTORY SAFE HARBORS: Trunk, locked glovebox, or behind rearmost seat (if no trunk).

An unlocked glovebox does NOT qualify. The compartment must be physically secured with a key or mechanical lock.

3. Operational Scope: Moving, Stopped, or Parked TPC § 49.031(b)

Open container laws apply on any public highway, street, or roadway right-of-way (including shoulders, curbs, and rest stop bays). The statute applies whether the vehicle is moving, stopped at a red light, idling, or parked with the engine switched completely off.

4. Penalties & CCP § 14.06 Cite-and-Release CCP § 14.06(b)

Standalone open container is a Class C misdemeanor (≤ $500 fine). Under CCP § 14.06(b) and TPC § 49.031(e), an officer cannot arrest the driver solely for open container. The officer must issue a written citation and release the motorist upon their written promise to appear.

5. DWI Open Container Enhancement: 6 Days Mandatory Jail TPC § 49.04(c)

If an individual commits DWI while having an open container in immediate possession, TPC § 49.04(c) escalates minimum county jail confinement from 72 hours to 6 continuous days (144 hours). This 6-day minimum is non-waivable and cannot be probated.

STANDALONE CITATION TPC § 49.031

Standalone Open Container: Class C Cite & Release

When a driver is sober but possesses an open container in the cabin, the offense is a standalone Class C Misdemeanor under Texas Penal Code § 49.031. An officer issues a written citation with notice to appear. The driver is not arrested, receives no jail time, and suffers no driver license suspension.

Vehicle Body Styles & Lawful Storage Guide

CHASSIS AUDIT

Texas Penal Code § 49.031(a)(2) establishes distinct compliance standards depending on whether the vehicle possesses a dedicated exterior trunk.

1. SEDANS (WITH TRUNK) TRUNK MANDATE

Must utilize the dedicated trunk. Placing an unsealed bottle on seats or floorboards is illegal because a trunk exists. Only the trunk or a locked glovebox provides immunity.

2. SUVS & HATCHBACKS BEHIND LAST SEAT

Lacking a trunk, TPC § 49.031(a)(2)(C) legalizes storage behind the last upright seat. If rear seats are folded flat, containers enter the prohibited cabin.

3. PICKUP TRUCKS BED OR TOOLBOX

The open bed is outside the passenger area. Open containers may be transported in the truck bed or locked toolbox. Cabin storage in crew cabs is strictly prohibited.

4. FLEET & RIDESHARE TPC § 49.031(c)

Passengers are exempt in limos, charter buses, and RV quarters. Uber/Lyft are NOT exempt. If a passenger opens alcohol, the driver can be cited.
