Texas Driving with Disabilities Program & Roadside De-Escalation Protocols

Roadside interactions with law enforcement demand rapid cognitive processing, clear communication, and deliberate physical actions. For motorists or passengers with diagnosed communication challenges—such as Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD), traumatic brain injury (TBI), speech apraxia, or severe hearing loss—sensory stimulation, flashing emergency lights, or auditory commands can provoke stress responses easily misinterpreted by officers.

To eliminate tragic misunderstandings, Texas established the pioneering Texas Driving with Disabilities Program (in collaboration with the Autism Society of Texas and the Governor's Committee on People with Disabilities). This voluntary program empowers drivers to securely disclose communication impediments through two official legal pathways: physical credential endorsement via Form DL-101 (Restriction Code 08) and vehicle registration linking via Form VTR-216 in the Texas Law Enforcement Telecommunications System (TLETS).

Roadside Encounter Protocols & Driver Safety Steps

When an officer initiates a traffic stop (flashing emergency lights/siren), following a disciplined sequence of steps ensures officer safety and de-escalates stress for all vehicle occupants:

01 • PULL OVER SAFELY

Signal & Stop on Right Shoulder

Activate your right turn signal and smoothly bring the vehicle to a stop on the far right shoulder or well-lit parking lot, completely clear of active traffic lanes.

02 • SECURE CHASSIS

Transmission in Park & Ignition Off

Shift transmission into Park, firmly set the emergency parking brake, turn off the engine, and activate hazard warning flashers.

03 • CABIN TRANSPARENCY

Interior Dome Light & Windows Down

Turn on the interior overhead dome light immediately if it is dark or overcast. Lower the driver's window completely (and passenger windows if tinted) so the officer can see inside.

04 • VISIBLE HAND POSITION

Hands Motionless at 10-and-2

Place both hands flat and motionless on the upper rim of the steering wheel. Instruct all passengers to keep their hands open and visible on their laps or seatbacks.

05 • VERBAL ANNOUNCEMENT

Inform Before Reaching for Credentials

Never make sudden movements or reach into pockets, glove compartments, or bags. Wait for the officer to request credentials, then verbally state where they are located before moving.

06 • REMAIN IN VEHICLE

Do Not Exit Without Direction

Remain seated inside the cabin with your safety belt fastened unless the peace officer explicitly requests or directs you to step out of the motor vehicle.

local_police Peace Officer Pre-Approach Accommodations TLETS TRIGGERED

When an officer queries a vehicle tag and receives a TLETS communication impediment return (or observes Restriction Code 08), Texas law enforcement training standards require tactical adaptations:

  • Extended Processing Time: Granting up to several seconds of silent processing latency before repeating a question, avoiding shouting or rapid demands.
  • Concrete Phrasing: Giving concise, sequential instructions rather than rapid compound commands.
  • Sensory Tolerance: Recognizing repetitive motor stimming, tactile fidgeting, or avoidance of direct eye contact as calming mechanisms rather than signs of deception or threat.
  • Alternative Communication Options: Utilizing written notes, texting, or specialized picture communication cards carried in the patrol vehicle.

Qualifying Diagnoses & Health Professional Authority

The Texas Driving with Disabilities Program covers a wide range of physical, sensory, and neurological conditions that impede verbal interaction with law enforcement:

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) NEUROLOGICAL

Sensory sensitivities, difficulty interpreting social cues, and delayed verbal response under stress. TLETS pre-approach alert informs officers to maintain a calm tone and avoid interpreting stimming as hostility.

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) & TBI TRAUMA / BRAIN

Exaggerated startle responses, hypervigilance, and cognitive memory fatigue. Accommodations include gentle approaches without shouting or sudden flashlight beams into the driver's eyes.

Severe Hearing Loss & Deafness AUDITORY

Inability to hear verbal commands shouted through car doors or windows. The program alerts officers to approach with written communication pads and visual cues rather than assuming the driver is ignoring commands.

Speech Apraxia, Dysfluency & Aphasia COMMUNICATION

Motor speech disorders or stuttering that may mimic slurred speech from intoxication. Pre-approach notice prevents wrongful field sobriety arrests based purely on speech dysfluency.
