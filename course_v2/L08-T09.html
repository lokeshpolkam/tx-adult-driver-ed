Duty to Stop & Render Aid (TTC § 550.021)

Fleeing the scene of an accident in Texas is a severe felony offense. The law imposes strict affirmative duties upon every involved driver:

Immediate Stop & Aid Mandate AFFIRMATIVE DUTY

Every driver involved in a collision resulting in injury or death must immediately stop at the scene (or as close as safely possible) and render reasonable assistance, including calling 911 and arranging transportation to a hospital if requested or if injury is apparent.

Hit-and-Run Involving Death SECOND-DEGREE FELONY

Failing to stop and render aid in a crash involving death is a Second-Degree Felony punishable by 2 to 20 years in state prison and a fine of up to $10,000.

Hit-and-Run Involving Serious Bodily Injury THIRD-DEGREE FELONY

Failing to stop in a crash resulting in serious bodily injury is a Third-Degree Felony carrying 2 to 10 years in prison.

Texas Form CR-2 & CR-3 Reporting

TX-DOT CRIS PORTAL

Texas documentation divides into two distinct administrative instruments:

Form CR-3: Texas Peace Officer's Crash Report $1,000+ MANDATE

Completed exclusively by sworn law enforcement officers who investigate the collision. Officers must file Form CR-3 electronically to TxDOT within 10 days if any person is injured or killed, or if apparent property damage to any vehicle or property totals $1,000 or more.

Form CR-2: Driver's Crash Report ("Blue Form") OPERATOR RECORD

Historically known as the "Blue Form," Form CR-2 is completed by vehicle operators when a crash is not investigated by law enforcement (e.g. minor parking lot fender benders under $1,000 damage). While TxDOT no longer formally archives CR-2 forms, completing a written crash statement remains vital for automotive insurance claims.

Mandatory Exchange Information (TTC § 550.023) LEGAL DUTY

Under TTC § 550.023, you must furnish: (1) Full legal name and home address; (2) Vehicle registration license plate number; (3) Automotive liability insurance company name and policy number; (4) Display your driver license upon request. Never apologize or admit fault at the scene; stick strictly to factual documentation.

Texas Towing Rights & Drop Fee Mandates

TEX. OCC. CODE CH. 2308

Following a collision, disabled vehicles are frequently removed by commercial wreckers. Know your statutory consumer rights under Texas law:

Driver's Right to Select Tow Operator

Unless a vehicle is creating an immediate traffic hazard or the driver is incapacitated, Texas law grants the motorist the legal right to designate their preferred towing company or request their insurance roadside service.

Mandatory Drop Fee Rule: Under Texas Occupations Code § 2308.254, if a tow truck operator has hooked up your vehicle but has not yet departed the private property or scene, they MUST release the vehicle upon payment of a regulated "drop fee" (typically $50 - $100) rather than hauling it to an impound lot.

Striking Parked Cars & Fixtures

TTC § 550.024

Colliding with unattended property requires specific procedural compliance under Texas law:

Striking an Unattended Vehicle WRITTEN NOTICE MANDATE

Under TTC § 550.024, if you strike a parked car, you must stop immediately and locate the operator or owner. If the owner cannot be located, you MUST leave a securely attached written note in a conspicuous place stating your name, address, and an explanation of the crash. Leaving without a note is a Class B misdemeanor if damage exceeds $200.

Striking Highway Fixtures / Landscaping TTC § 550.025

If you collide with a highway guardrail, signpost, utility pole, or roadside tree, you must take reasonable steps to notify the entity owning the property (e.g. TxDOT, city public works, or utility company) and report the incident.

Hit-and-Run Forensics & Paint Spectrometry

DPS CRASH LAB

Drivers who flee a collision scene under the delusion that they cannot be caught face sophisticated Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS) forensic technology:

Automotive Paint Chip Spectrometry OEM CHEMICAL SIGNATURE

Collisions leave microscopic paint transfer chips smaller than a pinhead. DPS crime labs use Fourier Transform Infrared (FTIR) spectrometry to analyze primer, basecoat, and clearcoat chemical layers, matching the exact automotive factory, year, make, model, and paint batch of the fleeing vehicle.

Lens & Plastic Debris Part Numbers DOT MOLD NUMBERS

Shattered fragments of headlamps, turn signals, and bumper covers contain microscopic molded DOT manufacturing part numbers. Investigators cross-reference these fragments to identify the precise vehicle trim and production year within hours of the crash.

Automated License Plate Readers (ALPR) TEXAS HIGHWAY CAMERAS

TxDOT traffic management centers and regional law enforcement operate extensive networks of high-speed ALPR cameras along highways, tollways, and intersections. Once a partial vehicle description is established, automated systems flag matching vehicles and trace the fleeing driver to their residence.

Move Over Law: Expanded Scope & Worker Protection

TTC § 545.157 EXPANSION

The Texas Move Over / Slow Down statute has been progressively expanded by the Texas Legislature to protect all roadside frontline workers:

local_police 1. Police, Fire & Emergency Medical Services (EMS)

The original core mandate protects law enforcement officers conducting traffic stops, paramedics treating crash casualties, and firefighters operating at incident scenes.

construction 2. TxDOT Highway Maintenance & Utility Service Vehicles

Expanded to include Texas Department of Transportation (TxDOT) road maintenance trucks, construction vehicles, electric/gas/telecom utility service trucks, and municipal garbage collection trucks displaying flashing amber or blue warning beacons.

handyman 3. Tow Trucks & Incident Management Vehicles

Commercial tow truck operators winching disabled vehicles on highway shoulders face mortal danger from passing traffic. Texas law treats stationary tow trucks with flashing lights with the exact same legal dignity and clearance mandates as marked state trooper patrol cars.

Crash Reporting: Texas Form CR-3

TX-DOT CRIS

Understanding state crash reporting criteria and official documentation:

The $1,000 Apparent Property Damage Benchmark

Under Texas Transportation Code § 550.062, law enforcement officers must investigate and submit an official Texas Peace Officer's Crash Report (Form CR-3) to TxDOT within 10 days if the collision resulted in:

  • Injury or death to any person; OR
  • Apparent property damage to any one person's vehicle or property of $1,000 or more.

Drivers can purchase certified copies of Form CR-3 through the TxDOT Crash Records Information System (CRIS) portal for insurance claims and legal defense.

TexasSure & Uninsured Motorist Safeguards

INSURANCE CODE CH. 1952

Understanding financial responsibility and electronic verification following a crash in Texas:

TexasSure Real-Time Insurance Database STATE VERIFIED

Investigating law enforcement officers do not merely rely on printed insurance paper cards; they verify policy active status electronically in real-time through the TexasSure automated financial responsibility database. Presenting a fraudulent or cancelled insurance card is a Class B misdemeanor.

Uninsured / Underinsured Motorist Protection (UM/UIM) CRITICAL COVERAGE

Under Texas Insurance Code § 1952.101, automotive insurers must offer Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage. In the event of a hit-and-run collision where the fleeing driver cannot be identified, your UM/UIM policy pays for vehicle repairs and medical costs that would otherwise fall on you.
