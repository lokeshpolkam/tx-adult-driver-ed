1. Statutory Prima Facie Speed Architecture (TTC § 545.352)

In the State of Texas, speed limits are established under the legal doctrine of prima facie evidence. Under Texas Transportation Code (TTC) § 545.352, operating a motor vehicle at a speed in excess of the statutory ceiling is prima facie evidence that the speed is not reasonable and prudent, and that the speed is unlawful. Unless an engineering and traffic investigation has determined and posted a different limit, the following statutory maximums apply automatically across Texas jurisdictions:

30 MPH Urban District

Applies to any street or highway in an urban territory characterized by built-up business, commercial, or residential structures situated at intervals of less than 100 feet for a distance of a quarter-mile or more (§ 545.352(b)(1)).

§ 545.352(b)(1)
15 MPH Urban Alleyway

Alleys are narrow passageways providing service access to the rear of buildings. Drivers face zero sightline margins, backing commercial vehicles, and emerging pedestrians. The speed ceiling is strictly 15 MPH (§ 545.352(b)(2)).

§ 545.352(b)(2)
15 MPH Texas Public Beaches & Access Roads

Under TTC § 545.352(b)(3), § 545.358, and Natural Resources Code Chapter 61 (Texas Open Beaches Act), Gulf Coast beaches are public highways. Speed is capped at 15 MPH because soft sand triples stopping distances and sunbathers possess complete right-of-way.

§ 545.358
70 MPH Numbered State & US Highways

Applies outside urban districts to highways numbered by the United States or the State of Texas (including FM and RM roadways). 70 MPH applies day and night for passenger cars, light trucks, and motorcycles (§ 545.352(b)(4)).

§ 545.352(b)(4)
60 MPH Non-Numbered Rural Highways (County Roads)

Applies outside urban districts to county roads not designated with a state or federal route number. Limits are set lower due to narrower pavement, blind crests, and unpaved shoulders (§ 545.352(b)(5)).

§ 545.352(b)(5)
60 / 50 MPH School Bus Statutory Ceilings

School buses operating on numbered highways that have passed a certified commercial vehicle inspection are limited to 60 MPH. School buses that have not passed commercial inspection or travel on non-numbered county roads are strictly capped at 50 MPH.

2. The Texas Basic Speed Rule (TTC § 545.351)

A critical legal concept in Texas jurisprudence is that posted speed limit signs do not grant permission to drive at that speed under adverse conditions. Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.351(a):

“An operator may not drive at a speed greater than is reasonable and prudent under the circumstances then existing.”

Furthermore, § 545.351(b) mandates that an operator shall control the speed of the vehicle as necessary to avoid colliding with another person or vehicle that is on or entering the highway in compliance with law and the duty of each person to use due care.

rainy Precipitation & Traction Loss

During heavy rainfall, water mixes with roadway oils. Driving 70 MPH on a posted 70 MPH highway during downpours violates § 545.351 because stopping distance escalates from 387 ft to over 600 ft. Drivers must reduce speed by at least one-third.

foggy Sightline & Darkness Bounds

If dense fog or darkness restricts forward visibility to 200 feet, driving at 60 MPH is an actionable statutory violation because bringing the vehicle to a halt requires 303 feet. Drivers must be able to stop within the assured clear distance visible ahead.

3. Minimum Speed Regulation & The Solomon Curve (TTC § 545.363)

While excessive speed is commonly recognized as hazardous, driving substantially below the prevailing traffic speed is equally lethal. Texas Transportation Code § 545.363(a) explicitly codifies:

“An operator may not drive so slowly as to impede the normal and reasonable movement of traffic, except when reduced speed is necessary for safe operation or in compliance with law.”

Under § 545.363(b), the Texas Transportation Commission, county commissioners courts, or municipal authorities are authorized to conduct engineering studies and establish posted minimum speed limits (e.g., 45 MPH on freeways with 70 MPH maximums). Operating below this posted minimum without mechanical necessity or traffic congestion is a punishable traffic misdemeanor.

Traffic Physics: The Solomon Curve U-Shaped Crash Probability

Pioneering research by David Solomon for the Federal Highway Administration proved that crash involvement rates do not rise monotonically with speed. Instead, crash risk follows a U-shaped curve based on speed variance. A vehicle traveling 15 to 20 MPH below the average traffic stream faces a collision involvement rate that is over 5 times higher than vehicles traveling at the median traffic velocity.

4. Special Texas Speed Corridors & Penalty Ladders

Texas statutes establish strict regulatory frameworks for unique corridors where vulnerabilities or engineering conditions demand modified operational caps and heightened statutory penalties:

Active School Zones (20 – 25 MPH) FINES DOUBLED (§ 542.404)

During designated morning arrival and afternoon dismissal hours when flashing yellow beacons operate, speeds are legally reduced to 20 or 25 MPH. Under TTC § 542.404, traffic fines are automatically doubled. Under TTC § 545.425, handheld wireless communication device use is strictly banned throughout active school crossing zones.

Highway Construction & Work Zones WORKERS PRESENT (§ 542.404)

Under TTC § 542.404, fines for speeding offenses in highway construction or maintenance zones are doubled if workers are present on the roadway. A speeding ticket that normally incurs $150 becomes a $300 fine. Violations in work zones with workers present cannot be dismissed by completing a driving safety course.

Move Over or Slow Down Law (TTC § 545.157) -20 MPH REDUCTION

When approaching stationary emergency vehicles, tow trucks, TxDOT maintenance vehicles, or utility service vehicles with flashing amber/blue/red lights on highway shoulders, drivers must: (1) vacate the lane closest to the vehicle if on a multi-lane road, or (2) reduce speed to 20 MPH below the posted limit (or to 5 MPH if the posted limit is under 25 MPH).
