Rail Safety & Highway-Rail Grade Crossings

Highway-rail grade crossings represent high-consequence kinetic intersections. A typical freight train weighing 12 million pounds traveling 55 MPH requires one full mile or more to stop. Texas Transportation Code § 545.251 mandates that drivers stop within 15 to 50 feet of the nearest rail whenever signals are active or a train is approaching.

The 3 Collisions of Every Crash:

Every motor vehicle crash consists of three distinct collisions: (1) Vehicle impact against the obstacle, (2) Occupant impact against the vehicle interior or restraint system, and (3) Internal organ impact against the skeletal structure. Safety belts spread force across strong bones (pelvis and ribcage) and extend stopping distance by precious milliseconds.

Railroad Crossbuck Sign

The white X-shaped crossbuck is a regulatory sign functioning as a Yield sign. If tracks have multiple rail lines, a number beneath the crossbuck specifies track count.

REGULATORY // YIELD TO ALL TRAINS
02

Flashing Lights & Gate Arms

Flashing red lights demand an immediate full stop between 15 and 50 feet. Driving around lowered, lowering, or closing gate arms is strictly illegal and extremely dangerous.

TTC § 545.251 // FULL STOP MANDATE
03

Blue ENS Emergency Sign

Mounted directly on the signal post or crossbuck. Contains the railroad company's emergency 1-800 dispatch telephone number and unique 6-digit + letter DOT crossing ID.

KEY STATUTORY TAKEAWAY
