Safe Following Intervals

Following distance is fundamentally a kinetic time buffer, not a static count of feet. Human optical perception cannot reliably gauge physical feet at highway velocities, but anyone can accurately measure rhythmic seconds against stationary roadside landmarks under Texas Transportation Code § 545.062.

TIMING METHOD 3 SECONDS

Stationary Landmark Timing

When the rear bumper of the lead car passes a fixed landmark (shadow, overpass, road sign), begin counting: "one-thousand-one, one-thousand-two, one-thousand-three."

Pass Threshold After Count 3
SPEED SCALING 4+ SECONDS

Highway Velocity Expansion

Under 30 mph in urban traffic, 3 seconds is baseline. At speeds exceeding 30 mph, Texas curriculum mandates expanding following interval to a minimum of 4 full seconds.

Speeds > 30 MPH 4 Sec Minimum
ADVERSE WEATHER 6–8 SECONDS

Rain, Ice, & Towing Multiplier

Wet pavement reduces tire traction by 50% or more. In heavy rain, fog, nighttime conditions, or while towing cargo trailers, expand your interval to 6 to 8 seconds.

Wet Pavement Double Cushion
MOTORCYCLE GAP 4–6 SECONDS

Rapid Deceleration Defense

Motorcycles stop much faster than cars due to low mass, and riders lack protective cages. Maintain 4 to 6 seconds following distance and leave a full car length when stopped behind at signals.

Stopped at Light 1 Full Car Length

Lesson Synthesis

Maintaining an assured clear distance is both a physics reality and a legal duty under TTC § 545.062. Measure following intervals in seconds using fixed landmarks, scale to 4+ seconds above 30 mph, and double your cushion in adverse weather.
