Administrative License Revocation & Implied Consent

Under Texas Transportation Code § 724.011, operating a motor vehicle on public roads constitutes legal implied consent to submit to chemical breath or blood testing if arrested for DWI. Refusal results in immediate civil Administrative License Revocation (ALR).

Statute 01 TTC § 724.011

The Implied Compact

Driving on Texas roadways is an agreement under state law that you will submit to chemical breath or blood testing when lawfully arrested for suspected driving while intoxicated.

TERMS AUTOMATIC CONSENT
Statute 02 CONTRAST

180d Refusal vs 90d Fail

Refusing a chemical test carries double the administrative suspension penalty: 180 days for test refusal versus 90 days for submitting and failing with a BAC of 0.08 or greater.

PENALTY REFUSAL DOUBLES BAN
Statute 03 TTC § 724.041

Strict 15-Day Appeal

Arrested motorists have exactly 15 calendar days from receiving Form DIC-25 to request an ALR hearing. Missing this deadline makes the driver license suspension irreversible.

DEADLINE 15 DAYS TO CONTEST
Statute 04 FINANCIAL

Reinstatement & SR-22

Reinstating an ALR-suspended license requires paying a $125 administrative fee to DPS and maintaining high-risk SR-22 insurance certificates continuously for up to 2 full years.

COST $125 FEE + HIGH PREMIUMS

QUESTION 1 OF 1 TX TRANS CODE § 724.011

What does the Texas Implied Consent law establish when a person operates a vehicle on public roads?

check_circle CORRECT ANSWER

Statutory Precept: Under Texas Transportation Code § 724.011, any person who operates a motor vehicle on a public highway or waterway is deemed to have given implied consent to submit to taking of one or more specimens of breath or blood for alcohol analysis if arrested for DWI.
