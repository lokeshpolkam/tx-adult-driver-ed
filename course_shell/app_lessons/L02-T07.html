Texas Driving with Disabilities: Communication Protocols

A voluntary state initiative that bridges the communication gap between law enforcement and motorists with diagnosed communication impediments (Autism, PTSD, Deafness, Speech Impairment), ensuring calm, informed roadside encounters before dialogue begins.

ROADSIDE PROTOCOLS // SECTION 03

Traffic Stop Interaction Standards

5 STEPS

Under the Texas Community Safety Education Act (SB 30) and TTC provisions, structured protocols govern officer-motorist interactions during traffic stops:

STEP 01 // PULLOVER local_police

Signal & Safely Stop

Activate right turn signal, smoothly decelerate to the right shoulder or well-lit parking lot, shift into Park, and shut off engine.

TX SB 30 / CSEA RIGHT SHOULDER
STEP 02 // POSITION front_hand

Visible Hands on Wheel

Keep hands clearly visible on the steering wheel (10-and-2 or 9-and-3). At night, turn on interior dome light to maintain clear cabin visibility.

COCKPIT VISIBILITY HANDS VISIBLE
STEP 03 // MOVEMENT pan_tool

Announce Before Reaching

Never make sudden reaches into the glovebox or pockets. Verbally inform officer: "My registration is in the glovebox; I am reaching for it now."

SAFETY PROTOCOL VERBAL NOTICE
STEP 04 // CODE 08 record_voice_over

Officer Communication

When Code 08 is displayed or TLETS alerted, the officer uses written communication pads, simplified questions, and provides extended processing time.

TTC § 521.142 ACCOMMODATION
STEP 05 // RIGHTS gavel

Civilian Rights & Duties

You must provide license and insurance upon lawful request. You have the right to remain calm, ask questions respectfully, and file compliments or grievances later.

By empowering officers with advance notice through TLETS and Code 08, roadside stops remain safe, dignified, and calm for all Texans.
