Legal Definition of Intoxication in Texas

Under Texas Penal Code § 49.01, intoxication is defined through a dual statutory standard: the objective per se 0.08 blood alcohol concentration threshold, or the subjective loss of normal physical or mental faculties from any intoxicating substance.

TDLR MANDATE · STATUTORY DEFINITION STANDARD

Texas law does not require proving a driver was staggering drunk. A motorist is legally intoxicated if they reach 0.08 BAC OR lack the normal use of mental or physical faculties due to alcohol, narcotics, pharmaceuticals, or any combination thereof.

Legal & Chemical Intoxication Framework

Four foundational principles governing forensic evidence, driver accountability, and Texas statutory enforcement.

Statute 01 PER SE LIMIT

Objective 0.08 Per Se

If forensic testing shows a BAC of 0.08 or higher, prosecutors do not need to prove erratic driving or mental incapacity. The chemical test result itself establishes criminal intoxication per se.

BURDEN AUTOMATIC GUILT THRESHOLD
Statute 02 SUBJECTIVE

Loss of Normal Faculties

A driver can be convicted of DWI with a BAC below 0.08 (e.g., 0.04 or 0.06) if officers establish loss of normal physical coordination, slurred speech, or slowed cognitive reflexes.

CRITERIA MENTAL / PHYSICAL TEST
Statute 03 PHARMACEUTICAL

No Prescription Defense

Holding a valid medical prescription provides zero defense against DWI under Texas law. If therapeutic pharmaceuticals impair your ability to safely control a vehicle, you are guilty of DWI.

DEFENSE ZERO RX IMMUNITY
Statute 04 METRIC UNITS

Statutory Chemical Units

Alcohol concentration is codified precisely: grams of alcohol per 210 liters of breath, per 100 milliliters of blood, or per 67 milliliters of urine sample under § 49.01(1).

balance Core Legal Takeaway

Texas Penal Code § 49.01 establishes two mutually independent routes to a DWI conviction. Having 0.08 BAC or above is automatic guilt per se. Alternatively, any loss of normal mental or physical control caused by alcohol or drugs constitutes intoxication, even if chemical testing is below 0.08 or refused.
