Factors That Contribute to Poor Decision Making

Adverse environmental conditions—flash floods, dust storms, dense fog, sun glare, and violent crosswinds—severely degrade driver judgment. Attempting to push through extreme Texas weather causes fatal miscalculations; reduced-risk drivers recognize natural limits and adapt immediately.

Turn Around Don't Drown

Texas leads the US in flash flood deaths. Two feet of raging water carries over 1,000 pounds of lateral force. Never risk your life or passengers for a shortcut.

02

West Texas Dust Storms

In a haboob, pull completely off paved surface. Shift to park, turn headlights and hazard lights OFF, take foot off brake, and stay securely buckled.

HABOOB // PULL OFF & LIGHTS OFF
03

Dense Fog Navigation

High beams scatter off moisture droplets, creating an impenetrable white wall. Use low beams only, guide along the right road stripe, and expand following gap.

FOG // LOW BEAMS MANDATORY
04

Crosswinds & Sun Glare

Strong gusts buffeting tall trucks on highway bridges can push vehicles out of lanes. At sunrise/sunset, polarized visors and immaculate glass prevent blindness.

KEY STATUTORY TAKEAWAY

Under TxDOT and National Weather Service mandates ("Turn Around, Don't Drown"), motorists must never attempt to cross flooded roads or low-water crossings. Moving water just 12 inches deep carries enough buoyant force to float and sweep away most passenger vehicles.
