Vehicle Maintenance for Safety — Tires, Brakes & Lights

Proper vehicle maintenance is an indispensable cornerstone of reduced-risk driving. Texas Transportation Code Chapters 547 and 548 mandate rigorous mechanical standards for tires, hydraulic brakes, and exterior lighting systems to prevent catastrophic mechanical failures at highway speeds.

Underinflation causes severe tire flexing, extreme heat buildup, and blowouts on hot Texas highways. Always inflate to the doorjamb placard specification, maintain at least 2/32" tread, and never ignore spongy or sinking brake pedals.

01

Doorjamb Tire Pressure

Always follow the vehicle manufacturer cold PSI sticker inside the driver doorjamb. The PSI number stamped on the tire rubber is the maximum bursting limit, not the operating pressure.

PRESSURE // DOORJAMB SPECIFICATION
02

2/32" Tread & Hydroplaning

Tires must possess at least 2/32" tread. Insert a penny with Lincoln's head inverted; if his entire head shows, the tire is legally bald and will hydroplane on wet pavement.

TREAD // 2/32" LEGAL MINIMUM
03

Hydraulic Brake Diagnostics

A pedal that feels soft, spongy, or sinks toward the floor indicates air bubbles in hydraulic lines or a dangerous fluid leak. Stop driving and have brakes serviced immediately.

BRAKES // HYDRAULIC PRESSURE INTEGRITY
04

360° Lights & Fresh Wipers

All headlights, turn signals, brake lights, and plate lamps must function properly. Replace dry-rotted wiper blades every 6-12 months to maintain visibility during sudden downpours.

KEY STATUTORY TAKEAWAY

Under Texas Transportation Code Chapters 547 and 548, vehicle operators are legally responsible for maintaining roadworthy tires (minimum 2/32" tread), responsive hydraulic brakes, clean wiper blades, and fully operational exterior lighting to protect all highway users.
