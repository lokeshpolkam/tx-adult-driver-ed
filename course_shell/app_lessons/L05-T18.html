Highway Environmental & Safety Profiles

Four critical operational dynamics for complying with Texas clean highway statutes and surviving roadside debris encounters.

Statute 01 CLASS C

Everyday Vehicle Litter

Discarding cigarette butts, beverage cans, or wrappers onto roads carries a criminal fine up to $500. Courts routinely sentence offenders to up to 60 hours of roadside cleanup service.

FINE UP TO $500 FINE
Statute 02 CLASS B

Major Illegal Dumping

Dumping trash between 5 lbs and 500 lbs escalates to a Class B misdemeanor, punishable by fines up to $2,000 and up to 180 days confinement in the county jail.

JAIL UP TO 180 DAYS JAIL
Statute 03 TTC CH. 725

Open Bed Load Covering

Under Texas Transportation Code § 725.021, all loose cargo transported in pickup beds or utility trailers must be secured with tarps, tie-downs, or nets. Allowing cargo to blow out is unlawful.

MANDATE TARP OR TIE DOWN

Avoid Panic Swerving

Over two-thirds of highway debris fatalities result from drivers violently yanking the wheel to evade light debris. Brake steadily in a straight line; never swerve blindly into adjacent lanes.

QUESTION 1 OF 1 TX HEALTH & SAFETY CODE § 365.012

Under the Texas Litter Abatement Act, what is the penalty for littering up to 5 pounds on a highway?

check_circle CORRECT ANSWER

Statutory Precept: Under Texas Health and Safety Code § 365.012(d), dumping or discarding litter of 5 pounds or less from a motor vehicle onto a public roadway or right-of-way constitutes a Class C misdemeanor punishable by a fine up to $500.
