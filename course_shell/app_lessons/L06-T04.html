Laws, Regulations & Penalties for Minors

Under the Texas Zero Tolerance law (Texas Alcoholic Beverage Code § 106.041), it is illegal for any motorist under 21 years of age to operate a motor vehicle with any detectable amount of alcohol in their system.

TX Alcoholic Beverage Code § 106.041: A minor commits an offense if the minor operates a motor vehicle or watercraft in a public place while having any detectable amount of alcohol in the minor's system.

Minor Statutory Accountability Framework

Four distinct legal mechanisms governing underage alcohol offenses, license revocations, and adult criminal exposure.

Statute 01 ZERO TOLERANCE

Any Detectable Trace

Minors do not receive the adult 0.08 BAC threshold. Operating a vehicle with a BAC of 0.01 or having the faint scent of alcoholic beverages on breath is sufficient for arrest under Texas law.

RULE ZERO ALCOHOL ALLOWED
Statute 02 ALR SYSTEM

License Revocation

First offense triggers a mandatory 60-day license suspension, 20-40 hours community service, and court-ordered completion of a state-certified alcohol awareness course within 90 days.

BAN 60-DAY MINIMUM SUSPENSION
Statute 03 ABC §§ 106.02-07

Non-Driving Sanctions

Possessing alcohol (MIP), attempting to purchase, or presenting a fake ID suspends the minor's driver license automatically for 30 to 180 days, even if the minor was walking on foot!

SANCTION LICENSE LOSS ON FOOT
Statute 04 TX PENAL § 49.04

Adult DWI Prosecution

If an underage motorist (age 17-20) exhibits a BAC of 0.08 or lost normal faculties, officers do not charge minor DUI—they charge full Adult DWI, with 72 hours mandatory jail lockup.
