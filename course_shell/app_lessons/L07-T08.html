Aggressive Driving & Road Rage Avoidance

Aggressive driving encompasses unlawful moving infractions like reckless speeding, tailgating, and improper passing. Road rage escalates into dangerous criminal conduct including assault, vehicle ramming, and threats. Mastering defensive disengagement and psychological self-regulation protects lives on Texas roadways.

Aggressive Driving & Road Rage De-escalation Protocol:

When confronted by an aggressive driver or persistent tailgater, never engage in competition, gestures, or retaliatory braking. Signal early, change lanes smoothly to the right, and allow the aggressor to pass. Avoid eye contact, remain calm, and maintain an assured clear distance (TTC § 545.062).

Avoid Eye Contact

Eye contact is interpreted as a direct challenge by an enraged driver. Look straight ahead, focus on traffic control devices, and maintain safety cushions.

Yield the Right-of-Way

Swallow your pride. Even if you legally possess the right-of-way, yield it to the aggressive motorist. Decelerate smoothly and let them speed ahead.

If a hostile vehicle follows you, never navigate to your residence or an isolated dead-end street. Lead them into full public view or law enforcement facilities.

Aggressive driving encompasses dangerous moving violations such as tailgating and erratic weaving, while road rage is a criminal act of violence, threats, or intimidation. Never engage or make eye contact with aggressive motorists. If followed, call 911 and proceed directly to a police station or hospital emergency room.
