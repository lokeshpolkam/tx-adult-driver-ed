Texas statute and driver safety doctrine mandate strict tactical steps to clear active travel lanes, evacuate occupants safely, and establish multi-channel visual warnings under TTC § 545.301 and § 547.305.

Breakdown Tactical Action Profiles

Four critical procedures for staying alive and protecting approaching traffic during high-speed highway breakdowns.

Priority 01 TTC § 545.301

Right Shoulder Relocation

Never stop in a live highway lane. Use momentum to glide onto the right paved shoulder, completely crossing the solid white fog line. Turn wheels away from travel lanes.

POSITION OFF PAVED ROADWAY
Priority 02 LIFE SAFETY

Passenger-Side Egress

Never step into live traffic from the driver door. Exit exclusively through the passenger door facing the grassy embankment. Stand behind the steel guardrail well away from car.

BARRIER BEHIND GUARDRAIL
Priority 03 TTC § 547.305

Distress Signaling

Activate 4-way hazard flashers immediately. Raise the engine hood to broadcast mechanical distress. If awaiting roadside assistance, tie a white cloth to driver door handle.

SIGNALS HOOD UP / HAZARDS ON
Zero Tolerance FATAL EXPOSURE

Left-Side Tire Hazard

Never kneel in an adjacent travel lane to change a driver-side flat tire on narrow shoulders. Call a professional tow truck or roadside service equipped with impact attenuators.

ACTION CALL TOW TRUCK

QUESTION 1 OF 1 TTC § 545.301

If your vehicle breaks down on a high-speed Texas highway, where must you position the disabled car?

check_circle CORRECT ANSWER

Statutory Rule: Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.301, drivers must maneuver a disabled vehicle entirely off the paved highway travel lanes onto the right shoulder or berm to prevent high-speed rear-end collisions.

Lesson Synthesis

Highway breakdowns demand prompt clearing of live lanes, offside passenger egress, immediate hazard beacon activation, and warning triangle deployment 100 to 200 feet upstream.

balance Core Legal Takeaway

Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.301, stopping on the main traveled part of a highway is illegal unless stopping off the roadway is impossible. Under the Texas Move Over / Slow Down Act (§ 545.157), approaching drivers must vacate the adjacent lane or slow down by 20 MPH below the speed limit when passing stopped emergency or disabled vehicles.
