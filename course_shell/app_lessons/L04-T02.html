Responding to Traffic Control Devices

Traffic control devices represent a statutory hierarchy engineered to eliminate collision risk and ambiguity in right-of-way. Under Texas law, drivers have an affirmative legal duty to identify, interpret, and correctly respond to electronic signals, posted road geometry, and roadway pavement markings.

SYSTEM HIERARCHY // SECTION 03

Device Classification & Driver Duties

4 CATEGORIES
TYPE 01 // RED LIGHTS traffic

Steady Red & Right-on-Red

Come to a full stop before entering crosswalk or stop bar. Right turn on red is permitted in Texas only after a complete stop and yielding right-of-way to oncoming traffic and pedestrians, unless prohibited by a posted sign.

TTC § 544.007(d) · MANDATORY FULL STOP
TYPE 02 // FLASHING BEACONS warning

Flashing Red vs. Yellow

Under TTC § 544.008, a flashing red signal functions exactly like an all-way stop sign: complete stop required before proceeding. A flashing yellow beacon requires reducing speed and proceeding with caution through the intersection.

TTC § 544.008 · BEACON PROTOCOL
TYPE 03 // STRIPING reorder

Pavement Markings

Broken lines indicate permitted lane crossing when safe. Solid white lines discourage lane changes. Solid yellow lines delineate opposing traffic. Double solid yellow prohibits passing in both directions.

TTC § 545.060 · LANE INTEGRITY
TYPE 04 // RAILROAD railway_alert

Railroad Grade Crossings

When electric flasher signals or gates are active at a railroad grade crossing, drivers must stop not closer than 15 feet and not farther than 50 feet from the nearest rail. Never stop on the tracks.

TTC § 545.157 · 15 TO 50 FT BUFFER

CORE PRINCIPLE: Texas traffic control devices establish mandatory legal boundaries. Steady red requires a complete stop; flashing yellow demands cautious deceleration; solid pavement markings restrict maneuvers; and railroad signals mandate a 15 to 50 foot safety buffer. Only direct orders from a police officer override these controls.
