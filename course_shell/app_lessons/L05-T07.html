TTC § 547.612 2/32" TREAD

Minimum Legal Tire Tread

Texas statute requires at least 2/32 inch of tire tread in every major contact groove. Tires below 2/32" cannot evacuate water, causing the tire to ride atop a film of water (hydroplane).

Inspection Threshold Penny Lincoln Test
WET SURFACES 2X DISTANCE

Wet Road Deceleration

Wet asphalt cuts tire friction in half and can double stopping distance. As speed increases above 35 mph, water pressure builds under worn tires, completely severing steering control.

Hydroplaning Speed Begins at 35 MPH
TTC § 547.302 160–200 FT

Overdriving Low Beams

Standard low beams illuminate 160 to 200 feet ahead. At speeds over 45 mph, your total stopping distance exceeds 200 feet. If an obstacle appears at the beam edge, a crash is mathematically guaranteed.

Low Beam Limit Max 45 MPH at Night

Lesson Synthesis

The basic speed rule (TTC § 545.351) requires adjusting speed to current road friction and visibility. Remember that doubling vehicle speed quadruples braking distance (4×), wet roads can double stopping distance, and tires require ≥ 2/32" tread to prevent hydroplaning.

balance CORE LEGAL TAKEAWAY

Under Texas law, the posted speed limit is valid ONLY under ideal weather, dry pavement, and clear daylight. Operating at the speed limit on wet roads or overdriving low-beam headlights at night violates TTC § 545.351 and exposes you to strict liability for collision damages.
