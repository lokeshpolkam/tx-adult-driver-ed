Oversize & Overweight Vehicle Safety

Texas Transportation Code Chapters 621 and 623 govern commercial transport of oversize, overweight, and superheavy loads. Sharing roadways with these massive rigs requires recognizing pilot escort cars, anticipating wide off-tracking turns, preventing the dangerous right-turn squeeze, and respecting extended braking distances.

Oversize Vehicle & Escort Pilot Convoy Rules:

Oversize and superheavy loads operate under Texas Transportation Code Title 7, Subtitle E (Chapters 621–623). Pilot escort cars with roof-mounted amber strobes warn motorists of loads exceeding standard 8.5-foot highway widths. Never cut between an oversize rig and its escort vehicles, and pass only when clear lane space exists.

Pilot Escort Vehicles

Equipped with amber lightbars, roof signs, and height measurement poles. When you see a pilot vehicle, reduce speed and never weave into the convoy gap.

02

The Right-Turn Squeeze

Because tractor-trailers cannot make tight turns without off-tracking, they swing left first. Passing on their right pins your vehicle against the curb.

TURNING // OFF-TRACKING SQUEEZE
03

Brisk Left-Side Passing

Always pass commercial trucks quickly on the left to minimize time spent in their expansive right-side No-Zone. Return only when both headlights are visible.

PASSING // 2-HEADLIGHT RULE
04

Downgrades & Escape Ramps

Descending steep Texas hills accelerates heavy trucks. Never stop or park on runaway truck ramps; they are vital arrestor beds for out-of-control braking failures.

balance Core Legal Takeaway

Pilot escort vehicles warn of superheavy or over-width transports. Never squeeze along the right side of a turning commercial truck, never cut in front until both headlights are visible in your center mirror, and give heavy rigs extensive braking cushions on highway descents.
