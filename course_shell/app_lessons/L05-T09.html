Texas Speed Limits & Special Corridors

Texas statutory law establishes default prima facie speed limits across distinct operational environments. Even in the absence of posted regulatory signs, motorists are legally bound by codified limits governing urban neighborhoods, public beaches, rural highways, and designated high-speed tollways.

Unless otherwise posted, Texas law mandates 30 mph in urban districts, 15 mph in urban alleys and public ocean beaches, 70 mph on numbered rural highways, and up to 85 mph on designated toll road facilities such as SH 130.

Urban Districts & Subdivisions

Unless a different limit is posted, 30 mph is the universal prima facie speed limit in Texas urban districts, residential neighborhoods, and municipal streets.

TTC § 545.352(b)(2-3) 15 MPH

Beaches & Urban Alleys

Operating speed along public Texas ocean beaches or inside urban alleys is restricted to a maximum of 15 mph to safeguard pedestrians, beachgoers, and blind property exits.

Gulf Beaches 15 MPH Strict Limit
TTC § 545.352(b)(4) 70 MPH

State & Federal Highways

On numbered state and federal highways outside urban districts, the prima facie limit is 70 mph for passenger cars. Texas eliminated separate night speed limits on state highways.

Day & Night Uniform 70 MPH
TTC § 545.353 85 MPH MAX

Texas SH 130 Tollway

TxDOT is authorized to establish speed limits up to 85 mph on designated toll roads designed for geometric high-speed travel, making it the highest posted speed in the Americas.

Lesson Synthesis

Texas speed limits operate on statutory prima facie standards: 30 mph in urban districts, 15 mph on beaches and alleys, 70 mph on numbered highways, and up to 85 mph on designated tollways.

balance CORE LEGAL TAKEAWAY

Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.352, statutory speed limits apply automatically even when no speed limit signs are erected. Driving in excess of 30 mph in an urban neighborhood or 15 mph on a public beach creates an immediate presumption of illegal operation.
