Signaling & Communication

Intent is the cornerstone of collision prevention. On public thoroughfares, safe trajectory execution requires broadcasting your planned velocity changes and path alterations before physical deceleration or turning begins. Texas Transportation Code § 545.104 mandates continuous signaling for at least 100 feet prior to any turn.

PILLAR 01 // 100-FT RULE turn_right

100-Foot Continuous Rule

Under TTC § 545.104, drivers must activate turn indicators continuously for at least 100 feet before turning or changing lanes. Tapping the indicator 10 feet prior or signaling while already inside the turn is unlawful.

TTC § 545.104 · 100-FT ADVANCE MANDATE
PILLAR 02 // HAND SIGNALS front_hand

Hand Signals for Equipment Failure

Under TTC § 545.107, if electric blinkers fail: Left turn = extend left arm straight horizontally; Right turn = extend left arm upward 90°; Stop/slow = extend left arm downward toward the road pavement.

TTC § 545.107 · MANUAL GESTURE LAW
PILLAR 03 // INTENT VS ROW sync_alt

Signaling Never Confers Right-of-Way

Activating a blinker merely broadcasts intention; it never obligates adjacent motorists to yield or gives you permission to force a lane merge. Drivers must always confirm blind spots before executing any lateral movement.

PILLAR 04 // HORN AUDIBILITY campaign

Horn Audibility & Hazards

Under TTC § 547.501, motor vehicle horns must be audible under normal atmospheric conditions from a distance of at least 200 feet. Use horns solely to prevent imminent collisions, not as an expression of annoyance.

CORE PRINCIPLE: Texas Transportation Code § 545.104 establishes an unconditional statutory duty: drivers must signal intent continuously for not less than 100 feet before turning or changing lanes. A turn signal broadcasts intention, but the driver retains the legal obligation to confirm blind spots and verify that destination lanes are clear.
