TTC § 544.007 · § 545.151

Traditional & Non-Traditional Intersections

Intersections represent the highest-risk conflict zones on Texas roadways. Electronic signal phases, multi-lane geometry, uncontrolled crossroads, and modern circular roundabouts govern statutory priority. Every driver must recognize who holds the legal duty to yield before crossing conflict points.

UNCONTROLLED GEOMETRY // SECTION 03

Roadway Topology Rules

4 TOPOLOGIES

When traffic signals, stop signs, or yield signs are absent, intersection geometry dictates legal priority under Texas law:

TOPOLOGY 01 // CROSSROAD grid_4x4

Uncontrolled 4-Way

Vehicle reaching intersection first enters first. On simultaneous arrival, driver on the left yields to driver on the right.

TTC § 545.151 YIELD TO RIGHT
TOPOLOGY 02 // T-JUNCTION call_split

Terminating Roadway

The driver on the terminating road must yield the right-of-way to all traffic approaching on the through highway or street.

TTC § 545.152 THROUGH ROADWAY FIRST
TOPOLOGY 03 // SURFACE TYPE terrain

Paved vs. Unpaved

A vehicle approaching from an unpaved (dirt, gravel, or caliche) roadway must yield to all vehicles on the paved highway.

TTC § 545.154 PAVED PRIORITY
TOPOLOGY 04 // MULTI-LANE alt_route

Divided Highway Crossing

When crossing a divided highway with a wide median, treat each half as a separate intersection and yield to oncoming lanes.

TTC § 545.151 SEPARATE CROSSINGS

verified CORE PRINCIPLE // SURRENDER PRECEDENCE

Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.152 and § 544.007, intersection safety relies on strict adherence to statutory priority. Whether facing an uncontrolled T-junction, a flashing yellow arrow, or a multi-lane roundabout, right-of-way must be surrendered to prevent conflicts. Never assume another driver will yield.
