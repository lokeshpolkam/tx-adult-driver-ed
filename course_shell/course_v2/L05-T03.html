The Physics & Legal Duty of Continuous Advance Signaling

Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.104(b), an operator intending to turn right or left shall signal continuously for not less than the last 100 feet of vehicle movement immediately before the turn. Signaling as you steer into the intersection, or tapping the blinker after initiating a lateral maneuver, constitutes an explicit statutory moving violation.

55 MPH (80.7 FT/S) 1.24 SECONDS Below average 1.5s reaction latency

The "False Intent" Trap: Activating your turn signal prematurely before passing an intermediate driveway or intersection misleads cross-traffic into believing you are turning prior to their position. Always pass all preceding driveways before engaging your 100-foot continuous blinker.

Immediate Signal Cancellation (TTC § 545.104(c)): An operator shall terminate the signal immediately after completing the maneuver. Leaving a blinker flashing induces following and opposing drivers into disastrous false-intent right-of-way assumptions.

Early Turn Lane Entry (DPS Handbook Ch. 6): On multi-lane thoroughfares, drivers must position themselves into the designated turn lane at least one-half block early (~150 to 200 feet). If you cannot safely reach the turn lane by this distance, you are legally obligated to proceed straight and circle the block rather than executing a hazardous last-second cut across active lanes.

car_crash CASE STUDY: THE FALSE-INTENT T-BONE COLLISION (WESTHEIMER RD, HOUSTON)

Driver A is cruising at 40 mph on an urban arterial intending to turn right at a signalized intersection 300 feet ahead. Driver A engages the right turn blinker 250 feet early. However, an un-signalized shopping center driveway sits 120 feet before the intersection. Driver B, waiting to turn left out of the shopping center driveway, observes Driver A's right turn signal, assumes Driver A is turning into the shopping plaza, and accelerates into the travel lane. Driver A continues straight toward the intersection and broadsides Driver B at 38 mph.

Legal Finding: Under Texas comparative fault doctrine (Texas Civil Practice & Remedies Code § 33.001), while Driver B failed to yield right-of-way, Driver A's premature false-intent signal contributed directly to the crash causation, barring full recovery and establishing contributory negligence.

Official Texas Hand & Arm Gestures & Hazard Flashers

When a motor vehicle's electrical signal lamps are inoperative, obscured by dirt, or washed out by glaring direct sunlight, Texas Transportation Code § 545.107 mandates that the driver communicate intended maneuvers using standardized hand-and-arm gestures from the driver's window:

LEFT TURN (180°)

Arm and hand extended straight horizontally beyond the window sill.

RIGHT TURN (90° UP)

Arm and hand extended upward at a right angle with open palm facing forward.

STOP / SLOW (90° DOWN)

Arm and hand extended downward at a right angle with palm facing rearward.

lightbulb MECHANICAL LAMP MANDATES & BRAKE LIGHT PULSING
  • • Mandatory Mechanical Signal Lamps (TTC § 545.106): Hand signals are legally prohibited and mechanical electric turn lamps are required if the distance from the steering post center to the left outside body edge exceeds 24 inches (2 feet), or if the distance from the steering post to the rear limit of the vehicle body/load exceeds 14 feet.
  • • Brake Light Pulsing / Deceleration Cue (TTC § 545.105): An operator shall not stop or suddenly decrease vehicle speed without first giving an appropriate signal. Tapping the brake pedal 2 to 3 times before applying full stopping pressure flashes the brake lamps, alerting trailing drivers before significant deceleration occurs.
warning EMERGENCY HAZARD FLASHER RESTRICTIONS (TTC § 547.501)

Four-way hazard flashers are legally restricted to vehicles that are disabled, stopped on the roadway or shoulder, or operating in an authorized emergency convoy/funeral procession. Cruising in ordinary rain with hazard flashers illuminated is unlawful in Texas because it overrides turn blinkers, masks brake lights, and creates severe cognitive confusion for trailing motorists.

build EMERGENCY ROADSIDE BREAKDOWN STAGING PROTOCOL
  1. Steer Completely Off Pavement: Pull as far onto the paved right shoulder as safely possible, ensuring the entire vehicle body clears the active travel lane by at least 3 feet.
  2. Activate 4-Way Hazard Lamps (TTC § 547.501): Immediately engage the hazard flasher switch to alert oncoming and trailing traffic from all directions.
  3. Triangular Reflective Warning Setup (TTC § 547.502): Place warning markers/flares at three calibrated distances behind the disabled vehicle:
    • Marker 1: 10 feet directly behind the vehicle on the traffic side.
    • Marker 2: 100 feet behind the vehicle centered on the shoulder.
    • Marker 3: 200 to 300 feet behind the vehicle to warn high-speed highway traffic before crests or curves.
  4. Occupant Safety Protocol: If on a high-speed interstate or during darkness, occupants must remain inside the vehicle with seatbelts securely fastened, or step behind the roadside metal guardrail barrier if safe to do so.
TEXAS HORN JURISPRUDENCE (TTC § 547.501): Every motor vehicle must have a horn audible from at least 200 feet under normal conditions. Texas law commands that a driver shall sound the horn only when reasonably necessary to ensure safe operation (preventing an immediate collision). Sounding the horn out of anger, road rage, or to vent frustration at traffic delays is prohibited.

swap_horiz LANE CHANGING • TTC § 545.060

Signaling Indicates Intent ONLY — Never Right-of-Way

A fundamental tenet of Texas traffic law: activating a turn signal does not grant right-of-way. Under TTC § 545.060, an operator on a roadway divided into lanes shall drive as nearly as practical entirely within a single lane and may not move from the lane unless that movement can be made safely.

visibility THE 4-STEP S.M.O.G. LANE CHANGE PROTOCOL
  • • S — Signal: Broadcast intention continuously for at least 100 feet prior to moving laterally.
  • • M — Mirrors: Check rearview mirror for trailing vehicles and side exterior mirror for adjacent lane clearance.
  • • O — Over-the-Shoulder Check: Perform a mandatory 90-degree chin-to-shoulder head check to inspect the B-pillar optical blind spot.
  • • G — Go Smoothly: Glide smoothly across the lane line into the target lane without sudden brake taps or hesitation, maintaining flow velocity. Cancel signal immediately upon arrival.
