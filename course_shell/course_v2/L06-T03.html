Adult DWI Penalties & Felony Escalation Ladder

Under Texas Penal Code Chapter 49, driving while intoxicated is adjudicated along a severe, non-discretionary statutory ladder. A standard first-time offense starts as a Class B misdemeanor carrying mandatory jail confinement, heavy fines, and administrative license suspension. However, specific aggravating factors—such as an excessive blood alcohol concentration (≥ 0.15), carrying a passenger under 15, or prior convictions—immediately escalate charges into Class A misdemeanors, state jail felonies, and second-degree prison felonies carrying up to 20 years in the Texas Department of Criminal Justice (TDCJ).

TEXAS CODES
FIRST OFFENSE DWI TPC § 49.04

Class B Misdemeanor DWI Mandates

A first-time driving while intoxicated conviction requires a minimum mandatory confinement of 72 hours up to 180 days in county jail, a fine not to exceed $2,000, and a driver license suspension of 90 to 365 days. If an open container of alcohol is located in the driver's immediate possession at the time of arrest, the minimum jail term elevates to 6 continuous days (144 hours) under TPC § 49.04(c).

JAIL: 72 Hours to 180 Days FINE: Up to $2,000
HIGH BAC ENHANCEMENT TPC § 49.04(d)

Aggravated DWI: Blood Alcohol ≥ 0.15

If a chemical breath or blood specimen reveals an alcohol concentration of 0.15 or higher at the time analysis is performed, the offense is elevated from a Class B to a Class A misdemeanor. Penalties surge to up to 1 full year in county jail, fines up to $4,000, and mandatory installation of a vehicle ignition interlock device (IID) as a condition of bond or probation.

JAIL: Up to 1 Year (365 Days) FINE: Up to $4,000
REPEAT ENHANCEMENTS TPC § 49.09

Second & Third DWI Penalties

A second DWI conviction is a Class A misdemeanor requiring 30 days to 1 year in jail, fines up to $4,000, a 180-day to 2-year license suspension, and mandatory ignition interlock. A third or subsequent DWI crosses into the felony threshold as a Third-Degree Felony punishable by 2 to 10 years in Texas Department of Criminal Justice (TDCJ) institutional division and fines up to $10,000.

2ND: 30d-1yr Jail (Class A) 3RD: 2-10 Yrs TDCJ (Felony 3)
STATE JAIL FELONY TPC § 49.045

DWI with Passenger Younger than 15

Operating a motor vehicle while intoxicated with a passenger younger than 15 years old constitutes a direct State Jail Felony, regardless of whether a crash occurred or any injury was sustained. Conviction results in 180 days to 2 years confinement in a state jail facility, fines up to $10,000, and automatic felony disenfranchisement.

CONFINEMENT: 180d to 2 Yrs FELONY FINE: Up to $10,000
INTOXICATION BODILY HARM TPC § 49.07 • § 49.08

Intoxication Assault & Manslaughter

Causing serious bodily injury while driving intoxicated constitutes Intoxication Assault (Third-Degree Felony, 2 to 10 years TDCJ prison). Causing the death of another human being by reason of intoxicated operation constitutes Intoxication Manslaughter (Second-Degree Felony, 2 to 20 years TDCJ prison, fines up to $10,000). Both offenses carry permanent, non-probated felony records.

ASSAULT: 2-10 Yrs (3rd Deg) MANSLAUGHTER: 2-20 Yrs (2nd Deg)

CHEMICAL TEST REFUSAL TTC § 724.035

First-Time Specimen Refusal: 180-Day Suspension

Refusing a lawful request for breath or blood testing under the Texas Implied Consent law results in an immediate 180-day administrative driver license suspension. Texas penalizes refusal twice as severely as testing over the legal limit (180 days vs. 90 days), and the refusal can be introduced as incriminating evidence at trial.

REINSTATEMENT: $125 Fee + SR-22 SOAH DEADLINE: 15 Calendar Days
STATUTORY FOUNDATION • IMPLIED CONSENT TTC § 724.011

Texas Transportation Code § 724.011: Consent to Taking of Specimen

Under TTC § 724.011 ("Consent to Taking of Specimen"), any person arrested for an offense arising out of acts alleged to have been committed under Texas Penal Code § 49.04 (DWI) is deemed to have consented to submit to the taking of one or more specimens of breath or blood for analysis to determine alcohol concentration or the presence of a controlled substance. Operating a motor vehicle on Texas public roadways establishes statutory consent under law upon lawful arrest.
