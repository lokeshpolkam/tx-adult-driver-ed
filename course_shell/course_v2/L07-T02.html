Crash Scene Management & Legal Duties

Statutory Step Sequencer, Freeway Clearance & Good Samaritan Immunity

Motorists involved in a crash on Texas roads face affirmative legal duties under Texas Transportation Code Chapter 550: immediate stopping, medical aid, moving drivable vehicles off freeways ("Steer It, Clear It"), exchanging four mandatory credentials, and police dispatch. The Texas Good Samaritan Law (TCPRC § 74.151) grants absolute civil immunity for good-faith emergency aid.

TCPRC § 74.151 • GOOD SAMARITAN LAW CIVIL SHIELD

Under TCPRC § 74.151(a), a person who in good faith administers emergency care at an emergency scene is not liable in civil damages for acts performed during the emergency. This shields volunteer rescuers providing first aid, CPR, or scene extraction.

cancel Four Statutory Disqualifiers (Immunity Voided):
  • Willful or Wanton Gross Negligence: Protection covers ordinary negligence; conscious reckless indifference voids the shield.
  • Remuneration / Fee Expectation: Care rendered for or in expectation of compensation is excluded (§ 74.151(b)(1)).
  • Business Solicitation: Persons present to solicit commercial services cannot claim immunity.
  • Producing Cause of Emergency: Under § 74.151(b)(2), a driver whose negligence caused the crash cannot claim immunity for aid to victims.
TTC § 550.022(b) • STEER IT, CLEAR IT FREEWAY MANDATE

If a crash occurs on a freeway main lane, ramp, shoulder, or median and each vehicle can be safely driven, operators shall move their vehicles as soon as possible off the main roadway to an investigation site or frontage road. Remaining in live lanes causes deadly secondary crashes and is a Class C moving violation.

TTC § 550.023 • MANDATORY CREDENTIAL EXCHANGE 4 CREDENTIALS

Drivers must exchange: (1) Operator name & address, (2) Vehicle registration & VIN, (3) Liability insurer & policy number, and (4) Display driver's license. Under § 550.024, if colliding with an unattended vehicle, drivers must locate the owner or leave a secure, visible written note.

TTC § 550.021 & § 550.022 • PENALTY LADDER SANCTIONS
  • Crash Involving Death (§ 550.021(c)(1)(A)): Second-Degree Felony (2 to 20 years prison, fine up to $10,000).
  • Serious Bodily Injury (§ 550.021(c)(1)(B)): Third-Degree Felony (2 to 10 years prison, fine up to $10,000).
  • Bodily Injury (§ 550.021(c)(2)): Up to 5 years TDCJ prison or 1 year in county jail, fine up to $5,000.
  • Property Damage ≥ $200 (§ 550.022(c)(2)): Class B Misdemeanor (up to 180 days jail, fine up to $2,000).
  • Property Damage < $200 (§ 550.022(c)(1)): Class C Misdemeanor (fine up to $500).
TTC § 550.026 • POLICE REPORT TRIGGERS FORM CR-3 CRIS

Immediate 911 notice is required if a crash causes injury, death, or inoperable vehicles. Note: Texas repealed driver written report Form CR-2 in September 2017 (SB 312). Officers file electronic reports (Form CR-3) via CRIS for damage > $1,000 or injuries.

STEP 1: STATUTORY STOP MANDATE TTC § 550.021 • § 550.022

Immediate Stop & Scene Security Mandate

Drivers involved in a collision must stop immediately at the scene without obstructing traffic more than necessary. Fleeing a crash involving death is a Second-Degree Felony (2 to 20 years prison); serious bodily injury is a Third-Degree Felony (2 to 10 years prison); property damage ≥ $200 is a Class B misdemeanor.

This legal shield protects ordinary rescuers performing first aid, bleeding control, or CPR.

Physical Scene Security & Medical Aid
  • Activate Hazards: Engage 4-way emergency flashers immediately upon stopping.
  • Deploy Triangles: Place reflective warning devices at 100 ft and 200 ft behind the crash scene.
  • Safe Refuge: Move behind guardrails; never stand between stopped cars or in live lanes.
  • Spinal Trauma Caution: Do NOT move injured victims unless there is immediate fire or submersion risk.
  • Direct Pressure: Apply direct pressure to control severe bleeding until EMS arrives.
