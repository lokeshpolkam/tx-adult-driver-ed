Community Safety Education Act (Senate Bill 30)

7-Step Interaction Protocol • Search Consent Jurisprudence • Officer Accountability

Enacted by the 85th Texas Legislature, the Community Safety Education Act (Senate Bill 30) codifies mandatory statewide standards governing interactions between motorists and law enforcement officers. Codified in Texas Education Code § 28.012, Texas Occupations Code § 1701.268, and the Texas Transportation Code, SB 30 establishes clear behavioral expectations, constitutional protections, operational duties, and procedural justice guidelines for both peace officers and citizens.

Constitutional Rights & Driver Legal Mandates Under SB 30

TTC § 521.025 • TPC § 38.02 CREDENTIAL DISPLAY

Drivers must display a valid driver license and proof of financial responsibility upon demand (TTC § 521.025, § 601.053). Giving a false name or date of birth while lawfully detained is a criminal offense under Texas Penal Code § 38.02 (Class B Misdemeanor).

U.S. CONST. AMEND. IV • SB 30 SEARCH CONSENT RIGHTS

Without a warrant or probable cause, officers require voluntary consent to search a vehicle. Drivers hold the constitutional right to decline consent by stating: "Officer, I do not consent to a search."

TPC § 38.03 • ZERO RESISTANCE CLASS A MISDEMEANOR

Under TPC § 38.03, physically resisting or obstructing a peace officer is a crime, even if the search is later ruled unlawful. Never physically touch or block an officer; challenge the search in court via a motion to suppress.

SB 30 DIRECTIVE UNMARKED STOP 9-1-1 PROTOCOL

If signaled by an unmarked vehicle in a dark corridor, activate hazard flashers, slow below the speed limit, dial 9-1-1 to confirm the officer with dispatch, and proceed to a well-lit public area.

STATUTORY COMPACT
01 // Vehicle Search Protections (Fourth Amendment)

Deny search consent if no warrant or probable cause exists: "Officer, I do not consent to a search." Zero physical resistance under TPC § 38.03.

02 // Right to Remain Silent vs ID (Fifth Amendment)

Refuse exploratory questions, but you MUST display your driver license under TTC § 521.025. False ID is a Class B misdemeanor (TPC § 38.02).

03 // Recording the Interaction (First Amendment)

You have the legal right to record audio/video from within your vehicle. Keep hands visible and never obstruct the officer.

01 // Immediate Halt Mandate (TTC § 545.156)

Immediately pull to right shoulder. Shift to Park, set emergency brake, turn off engine, engage hazards, and illuminate dome light at night.

02 // Mandatory Document Display (TTC § 521.025 / § 601.053)

Mandatory to present valid driver license and proof of liability insurance upon demand of a peace officer.

03 // Hand Anchorage & Cockpit Discipline

Remain inside the car with hands visible at 10 and 2 on the steering wheel. Verbalize before reaching into glovebox or pocket.

01 // Roadside De-Escalation Axiom

Never argue citation validity on the shoulder. The court is the legal venue to contest charges, not the roadside.

02 // Identification & Sworn Complaints (Tex. Gov't Code § 614.022)

Note officer name, badge, agency, and car unit number. Complaints must be in writing and signed under oath with Internal Affairs.

03 // Signing Citation (TTC § 543.005)

Signing is strictly a promise to appear, NOT an admission of guilt. Refusal mandates immediate custodial arrest.
