STATUTORY FRAMEWORK • TTC CH. 724 & CH. 524

TEXAS LAW
1. Texas Implied Consent Doctrine & Arrest Prerequisite TTC § 724.011

Under Texas Transportation Code § 724.011, any person who operates a motor vehicle on a public highway or watercraft is deemed to have given consent to submit to the taking of one or more specimens of breath or blood for chemical analysis. However, this statutory consent is only activated if the motorist is lawfully arrested by an officer possessing probable cause that the driver was operating while intoxicated.

2. Statutory Admonition: Texas DPS Form DIC-24 TTC § 724.015

Before requesting a breath or blood specimen, the peace officer must verbally read and provide a written copy of statutory warnings (Texas DPS Form DIC-24). The admonition explicitly informs the driver that: (1) refusal will result in license suspension of at least 180 days; (2) refusal can be admitted in court; and (3) testing ≥ 0.08 BAC will result in an automatic 90-day license suspension.

3. Physical License Confiscation & Form DIC-25 TTC § 724.032

When an arrested driver refuses testing or provides a failing specimen, the officer immediately confiscates the physical plastic driver license and issues Form DIC-25 (Notice of Suspension and Temporary Driving Permit). This form acts as a valid temporary driving permit for exactly 40 calendar days from the date of arrest, allowing the driver to operate lawfully while pursuing administrative relief.

4. The Strict 15-Calendar-Day SOAH Hearing Cutoff TTC § 724.041

To contest the administrative license suspension, the motorist or their attorney must file a formal written request for an evidentiary hearing before the State Office of Administrative Hearings (SOAH) within strictly 15 calendar days after receiving Form DIC-25. If the 15-day deadline is missed, the right to contest is permanently forfeited, and the suspension takes effect on Day 40.

5. Day 40 Automatic Suspension & Reinstatement TTC § 521.3466

On the 40th calendar day after notice, the temporary permit expires and the administrative suspension officially activates. Driving after Day 40 without an Occupational Driver License (ODL) constitutes Class B misdemeanor Driving While License Invalid (DWLI). Reinstatement requires paying a $125 administrative fee to Texas DPS and filing proof of financial responsibility (Form SR-22 insurance) for up to two continuous years.

ADMINISTRATIVE JURISPRUDENCE

SOAH Administrative Law & Civil Dual Track

SOAH CODEX

An ALR proceeding is completely independent of criminal prosecution for DWI. In Texas, a motorist faces two separate tracks simultaneously: civil administrative license revocation and county criminal court.

1. DUAL-TRACK PROCEEDINGS INDEPENDENT

The criminal court decides guilt, jail, and fines beyond a reasonable doubt. The SOAH hearing determines license validity by a preponderance of the evidence. Acquittal in criminal court does not automatically invalidate an ALR civil suspension.

2. SOAH EVIDENTIARY ISSUES TTC § 724.042

At the hearing, DPS must prove: (1) reasonable suspicion to stop; (2) probable cause to arrest for DWI; (3) statutory warnings were provided; and (4) the motorist refused or failed the test. If DPS fails on any point, driving privileges are fully restored.

3. OCCUPATIONAL LICENSE (ODL) TTC § 521.242

Suspended drivers may petition a court for an Occupational Driver License (ODL) to drive for work, school, or essential household chores up to 12 hours/day. First offenders have no waiting period; repeat offenders face hard suspension blackouts of 90 to 180 days.

4. RELICENSURE & SR-22 FINANCIAL MANDATE

Following suspension, relicensure requires paying a $125 administrative fee to DPS and maintaining continuous SR-22 high-risk insurance filing for up to 2 years. Failure to maintain active SR-22 triggers immediate secondary re-suspension.
