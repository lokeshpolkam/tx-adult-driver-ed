Pavement Markings & Striping Standards

Longitudinal Lines, TWLTL Reservoirs & Reversible Corridors

Pavement markings are official traffic-control devices painted on road surfaces that direct vehicle trajectory, assign right-of-way, and establish spatial boundaries under the Texas Transportation Code (TTC) and the Texas Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices (TMUTCD).

Road markings communicate through a standardized visual language of colors and patterns: Yellow lines always separate opposing flows of traffic on two-way roadways or designate the left road edge on divided highways. White lines separate traffic moving in the same direction or designate the right road shoulder (fog line). Broken lines authorize crossing when maneuvers can be made safely, whereas solid lines discourage or prohibit crossing.

Under Texas law, drivers must drive on the right half of the roadway. Crossing double solid yellow lines to pass is strictly prohibited under TTC § 545.055. Longitudinal Markings Taxonomy

Summary of Texas road marking patterns, color designations, and legal mandates:

Broken Yellow Line TTC § 545.051

Separates traffic traveling in opposite directions. Passing to the left is permitted when the oncoming lane is clear of traffic for a safe distance (sight distance ≥ 500 ft). You must return to your right lane before coming within 200 feet of an approaching vehicle.

Solid Yellow Line (Next to Broken) TTC § 545.055

If the solid yellow line is on your side, passing is prohibited. If the broken yellow line is on your side, passing is permitted when oncoming traffic is clear.

Double Solid Yellow Lines NO PASSING

Strict barrier equivalent prohibiting passing from both directions. Key Exception (TTC § 545.055(b)): You may lawfully cross double solid yellow lines to complete a left turn into or from an alley, private road, or driveway after yielding to oncoming traffic.

Broken White Line TTC § 545.060

Separates lanes moving in the same direction. Lane changes are permitted when safe. Texas law mandates signaling at least 100 feet in advance (TTC § 545.104).

Solid & Double Solid White Lines TMUTCD § 3B.04

A single solid white line channelizes same-direction traffic and discourages lane changing (freeway exit queues, tunnels). A double solid white line strictly prohibits lane changes (e.g., HOV/express toll buffer zones). Solid white also designates the right shoulder edge.

TWLTL & Reversible Corridor Rules

Critical Texas operational constraints for complex urban multilane corridors:

turn_left Two-Way Left Turn Lane (TWLTL) • TTC § 545.060

The center lane marked with solid outer and broken inner yellow lines is designed solely for vehicles turning left. Under Texas law and TxDOT standards:

  • Never Use for Passing: You may never use a TWLTL as an express passing or overtaking lane.
  • 200-Foot Rule: Enter the lane only within ~200 feet (about one-half city block) of your intended turn. Traveling continuously in the TWLTL is an unlawful moving violation.
  • Two-Stage Driveway Turn: When turning left from a private driveway onto a busy multilane street, you may cross through traffic, pause in the TWLTL as a safe refuge, wait for a gap in oncoming traffic, and merge smoothly.
swap_vert Reversible Lanes & Overhead Gantry Signals • TTC § 544.009

Reversible lanes carry traffic in one direction during morning rush hour and the opposite direction during evening rush hour. Marked by double broken yellow lines on each side, they are strictly controlled by overhead signals:

↓ GREEN ARROW Lane open; you may drive in this lane.
✗ STEADY YELLOW X Lane is terminating; vacate lane safely.
✗ FLASHING YELLOW X Use lane only to make a left turn.
✗ STEADY RED X Lane closed; entering is a misdemeanor.
straighten Transverse Markings & Improved Shoulders

Stop Lines (12–24 in): You must stop your vehicle completely before the front bumper crosses the white stop line (TTC § 544.010).
Yield Lines ("Shark's Teeth"): Rows of white solid triangles pointing toward oncoming traffic indicate where you must yield right-of-way.
Driving on Improved Shoulders (TTC § 545.058): You may drive on an improved right shoulder to pass a vehicle that is stopped or turning left, to decelerate before turning right, to accelerate before merging, or to avoid a collision. You may never use it to pass a vehicle traveling straight ahead.
