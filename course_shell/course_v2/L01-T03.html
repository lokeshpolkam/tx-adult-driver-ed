Driving is a Privilege

Risks, Responsibilities, Statutory Obligations & Criminal Consequences

Operating a motor vehicle on Texas roadways is not a constitutionally protected right, but a conditional legal privilege granted by the State of Texas through the Department of Public Safety (DPS). This privilege is continually conditioned upon strict adherence to statutory duties, financial responsibility mandates, and emergency safety protocols.

verified_user Texas DPS Revocable Authority:

Under Texas Transportation Code §§ 521.291–521.294, the Department of Public Safety possesses continuous statutory authority to suspend, revoke, cancel, or deny the driver license of any motorist who fails to maintain financial responsibility, accumulates habitual violations, or demonstrates disregard for public safety.

HABITUAL VIOLATOR SYSTEM TTC § 521.292

Driver License Moving Violation Point Accumulation

Texas tracks moving violation convictions on driver records. Point scale: 2 points for standard moving violation; 3 points if moving violation results in a collision. CURRICULUM // CORE STATUTES
TTC §§ 545, 547, 550
MANDATORY STATUTORY CODEX

Child Restraints, Lighting & Crash Scene Duties

CHILD RESTRAINT MANDATE TTC § 545.412

Any child younger than 8 years of age must be secured in an approved child passenger safety seat system according to manufacturer directions, UNLESS the child is at least 4 feet 9 inches (57 in) in height. Violations carry a fine of $25 to $250.

ADULT SEAT BELTS (TTC § 545.413): Mandatory for ALL occupants regardless of seating position. Driver is cited for unrestrained passengers under 17.
MANDATORY LIGHTING HOURS TTC § 547.302

Headlights are required from 30 minutes after sunset to 30 minutes before sunrise, and whenever atmospheric conditions (rain, fog, smog) reduce visibility to less than 1,000 feet ahead.

BEAM DIMMING (TTC § 547.333): Dim high beams within 500 ft of oncoming traffic and within 300 ft when following.
FELONY HIT & RUN DUTIES TTC § 550.021

Drivers in a crash involving injury or death must immediately stop, remain at the scene, render reasonable assistance (call 911), and exchange information (§ 550.023).

CRIMINAL SANCTIONS: Crash involving death is a 2nd-Degree Felony (2–20 yrs prison). Serious injury is a 3rd-Degree Felony (2–10 yrs prison). Conviction results in automatic driver license revocation (§ 521.341).
HABITUAL VIOLATOR SUSPENSION TTC § 521.292

DPS will administratively suspend the operating privilege of any motorist convicted of 4 or more moving violations within 12 months, or 7 or more moving violations within 24 months. Drivers have 20 days from notice to request an administrative hearing.
