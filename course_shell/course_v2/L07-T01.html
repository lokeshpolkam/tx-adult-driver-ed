Vulnerable Road Users & Crosswalk Jurisprudence

Multi-Modal Ecosystem • Criminal Negligence Penalties • Kinematic Lethality

Texas public roadways are shared by multi-ton passenger cars, commercial 18-wheelers, pedestrians, cyclists, motorcyclists, and animal-drawn carriages. Vulnerable Road Users (VRUs) possess zero structural armor: in Texas, pedestrian and bicycle fatalities represent one out of every five roadway deaths (~850 to 900+ deaths annually). Under Texas Transportation Code Chapter 552 and the Lisa Torry Smith Act (Senate Bill 1055, TTC § 545.428), motorists are held to a strict affirmative duty of due care, and failing to yield to a vulnerable road user in a crosswalk with criminal negligence is a felony.

Crosswalk Right-of-Way & Criminal Negligence Penalties

Under Texas Transportation Code Chapter 552, a crosswalk exists at every intersection of public highways connecting the lateral curb lines, whether marked by white thermoplastic stripes or completely unmarked. Motorists frequently misunderstand the legal scope of crosswalk rights, resulting in preventable fatal pedestrian impacts.

TTC § 552.003(a) PEDESTRIAN RIGHT-OF-WAY

The operator of a vehicle shall stop and yield the right-of-way to a pedestrian crossing a roadway in a marked or unmarked crosswalk if: (1) no traffic-control signal is in place or operation; and (2) the pedestrian is on the half of the roadway in which the vehicle is traveling, or is approaching so closely from the opposite half as to be in danger.

TTC § 552.003(c) • STRICT BAN PASSING PROHIBITION

If a vehicle is stopped at a marked or unmarked crosswalk at an intersection to permit a pedestrian to cross, the operator of another vehicle approaching from the rear may not overtake and pass the stopped vehicle. Passing a vehicle that has yielded to a pedestrian is one of the most deadly moving violations in Texas, frequently causing blind side pedestrian strikes.

LISA TORRY SMITH ACT (TTC § 545.428) CRIMINAL NEGLIGENCE

Passed as Senate Bill 1055, this statute closed the loophole where motorists who struck pedestrians in crosswalks received only minor traffic tickets. Under § 545.428, an operator who with criminal negligence fails to yield to a vulnerable road user in a crosswalk commits:

  • Bodily Injury: Class A Misdemeanor (Fine up to $4,000, up to 1 yr jail).
  • Serious Bodily Injury: State Jail Felony (180 days to 2 yrs state jail, fine up to $10,000).
WHITE CANE LAW • TTC § 552.010 HRC § 121.007

An operator of a vehicle approaching a pedestrian who is blind or visually impaired and carrying an all-white cane (or white cane with red tip) or using a guide dog must come to a complete stop and take all necessary precautions to avoid injuring the pedestrian. A blind pedestrian has unconditional statutory precedence.

VRU CLASS: PEDESTRIANS TTC § 552.003 • § 552.010

Pedestrian Rights, Crosswalks & White Cane Mandate

Drivers must yield to pedestrians in marked or unmarked crosswalks (TTC § 552.003). Passing a vehicle stopped for a pedestrian is strictly prohibited (§ 552.003(c)). When approaching a blind pedestrian carrying a white cane or using a guide dog, drivers must come to a complete, unconditional stop (TTC § 552.010 & TX Human Resources Code § 121.007).

Bicycles, Motorcycles & Rural Conveyances

Texas Transportation Code Title 7 classifies cyclists as vehicle operators while providing strict protective statutory envelopes. Additionally, rural highways frequently encounter slow-moving agricultural machinery and equestrians who enjoy full statutory roadway entitlements.

BICYCLE SUBSTANDARD LANE DOCTRINE TTC § 551.103(a)(4)

While cyclists are generally directed to ride near the right curb, TTC § 551.103 explicitly permits cyclists to occupy the center of the lane if the lane is substandard in width (less than 14 feet wide without an adjacent marked bike lane). Motorists must never attempt to squeeze alongside a cyclist in a narrow lane; you must wait until it is safe and change lanes to pass.

SAFE PASSING BUFFER ORDINANCES 3 FT (CARS) / 6 FT (TRUCKS)

Over 30 Texas municipalities have enacted explicit Vulnerable Road User ordinances requiring motorists to provide at least 3 feet of lateral clearance when passing bicycles (expanding to 6 feet for commercial trucks). Passing too closely can startle cyclists or destabilize them with aerodynamic buffeting.

EQUESTRIANS & SLOW-MOVING VEHICLES TTC § 542.003 • § 547.703

Under TTC § 542.003, horse riders and horse-drawn carriages have full legal rights to use Texas highways. Never sound your horn or rev your engine when approaching horses; spooking an animal can cause fatal overturns. Farm equipment moving at 25 mph or slower must display the triangular fluorescent orange/red SMV emblem (§ 547.703).
