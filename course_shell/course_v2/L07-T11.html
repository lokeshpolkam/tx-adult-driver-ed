Cooperating with Law Enforcement & Protecting Vulnerable Road Users

MOVE OVER / SLOW DOWN ACT • EMERGENCY CORRIDORS • WORK ZONE FINES • WHITE CANE MANDATE

Operating safely on Texas roadways requires strict adherence to statutory duties when interacting with emergency responders, law enforcement peace officers, highway maintenance crews, and vulnerable road users. Under the Texas Move Over / Slow Down Act (TTC § 545.157, expanded under HB 898), motorists approaching stationary emergency vehicles, TxDOT work crews, or authorized tow trucks must either vacate the adjacent lane or decelerate 20 MPH below the posted limit. Furthermore, Texas law imposes an absolute duty to stop for visually impaired pedestrians carrying white canes (Tex. Hum. Res. Code § 121.007) and affords equal vehicular rights and safe passing clearances to bicyclists and motorcyclists.

Cooperating Lawfully During Police Traffic Stops

Standardized 6-step roadside safety sequence codified under the Texas Community Safety Education Act (SB 30) and Texas Transportation Code § 545.156.

1
Pull Safely to Right Shoulder: Signal right and decelerate smoothly to the right shoulder or well-lit parking lot. Avoid stopping in active traffic lanes or on bridges.
2
Park, Emergency Brake & Hazards: Shift into PARK, set emergency brake firmly, shut off the engine, and activate hazard warning flashers to alert approaching traffic.
3
Cabin Dome Light at Night: At night or in low light, turn on the interior dome light so the approaching peace officer can clearly observe the cabin interior.
4
Hands Visible at 10 and 2: Roll driver window down and place both hands flat on top of the steering wheel. Instruct all passengers to keep hands visible.
5
Announce Before Reaching: State where documents (DL, insurance) are located before reaching. Disclose immediately if transporting any legal firearm.
6
Unmarked Stops Protocol: Turn on flashers, drive slowly, dial 9-1-1 on speaker to confirm officer identity with dispatch, and stop at a populated location.

MANDATORY LANE VACATION TTC § 545.157(a)(1)

Vacate the Closest Lane on Multi-Lane Highways

When approaching a stationary emergency vehicle, police car, tow truck, or TxDOT work truck with flashing lights, drivers on highways with two or more lanes in the same direction MUST vacate the lane closest to the stopped vehicle if road and traffic conditions allow safe lane changes.

ABSOLUTE STATUTORY STOP MANDATE TEX. HUM. RES. CODE § 121.007

Blind Pedestrians with White Canes or Guide Dogs

Under Texas Human Resources Code § 121.007, a driver approaching a totally or partially blind pedestrian carrying a white cane (with or without a red tip) or guided by an assistance dog MUST bring their vehicle to a COMPLETE STOP and take all necessary precautions to avoid injury. Failure to stop is a Class C misdemeanor.

ACTION: 100% Complete Stop PRECEDENCE: Absolute Priority

Absolute Precedence for Visually Impaired Pedestrians

Whenever a pedestrian carrying an extended white cane (with or without a red tip) or accompanied by a certified guide dog approaches or enters the roadway, drivers MUST bring their vehicle to a complete stop regardless of traffic signals or crosswalk markings.

Bicycle Passing Clearances & Motorcycle Buffer Zones

Bicycles are legal vehicles with equal rights to roadway space. When turning right across bicycle lanes, merge into the lane prior to turning rather than cutting across the cyclist's path. Always maintain an expanded 4 to 6-second trailing cushion behind motorcycles.
