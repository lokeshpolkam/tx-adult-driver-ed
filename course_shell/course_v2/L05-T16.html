TTC § 544.008 • § 545.151

Traffic Control & Signal Jurisprudence / Intersection Priority

Highway transportation systems operate as dynamic kinetic networks governed by strict statutory priority hierarchies. Under the Texas Transportation Code, every signal phase, lane convergence, and intersection geometry imposes mandatory legal duties designed to prevent conflicting vehicular trajectories from intersecting. From the strict legal equivalency of a flashing red signal to an all-way stop under TTC § 544.008, to right-of-way arbitration at uncontrolled cross-streets, terminating T-intersections, and high-speed freeway ramp throats (TTC § 545.153), mastering systemic traffic jurisprudence is foundational to defensive driving and crash prevention.

TTC § 544.008 • FLASHING SIGNALS FULL STOP / CAUTION

(a) Flashing Red Signal: An operator facing an illuminated flashing red light shall stop at a clearly marked stop line, or before entering a crosswalk on the near side of the intersection, or at the point nearest the intersecting roadway where the operator has a view of approaching traffic. The right to proceed is subject to the rules applicable after stopping at a stop sign.
(b) Flashing Yellow Signal: An operator facing an illuminated flashing yellow signal may proceed past the signal only with caution, covering the brake and yielding to any vehicle or pedestrian already in the intersection.

• Critical Enforcement Doctrine:
Rolling through a flashing red light carries the identical criminal penalty as running a solid red signal or octagonal stop sign (Class C misdemeanor moving violation under TTC § 542.401).
TTC § 545.151 & § 545.152 • INTERSECTION ARBITRATION PRIORITY CODEX

§ 545.151 (Uncontrolled Intersections): An operator approaching an intersection not controlled by an official traffic-control device shall yield right-of-way to a vehicle that has entered the intersection from the operator's right, or that is approaching the intersection on the operator's right close enough to constitute an immediate hazard.
§ 545.152 (Vehicle Turning Left): An operator intending to turn left within an intersection or into an alley, private road, or driveway shall yield right-of-way to any vehicle that is approaching from the opposite direction and that is in the intersection or so close as to present an immediate hazard.

TTC § 545.060 • DRIVING ON ROADWAY LANED FOR TRAFFIC LANE DISCIPLINE

(a) Single Lane Maintenance: An operator on a roadway divided into two or more clearly marked lanes for traffic shall drive as nearly as practical entirely within a single lane and may not move from the lane unless that movement can be made safely.
(b) Safe Lane Changes & Prohibited Straddling: Texas jurisprudence strictly prohibits straddling painted lane lines, erratic lane weaving, and unsafe lane departures. Drivers must continuously signal intent for at least 100 feet (TTC § 545.104) and confirm clearance before leaving a marked travel lane.

• Statutory Enforcement Standard:
Straddling lane markers or drifting out of a marked lane under TTC § 545.060 establishes reasonable suspicion for traffic stops and carries Class C moving violation fines under TTC § 542.401.
TTC § 545.104 • 100-FOOT SIGNAL MANDATE INTENT COMMUNICATION

An operator intending to turn a vehicle right or left, or change travel lanes, shall signal continuously for not less than the last 100 feet traveled by the vehicle before the turn. Initiating an abrupt turn signal simultaneously with the steering wheel turn violates Texas law and denies trailing high-speed motorists sufficient perception-reaction buffer time ($t_{pr} \approx 1.5\text{ s}$).

TTC § 545.153(c) • FRONTAGE ROAD YIELD MANDATE FREEWAY ACCESS

An operator on an access or frontage road of a controlled-access highway shall yield the right-of-way to a vehicle that is entering or about to enter the access road from the highway, or that is leaving or about to leave the access road to enter the highway. High-speed freeway exit traffic cannot decelerate within short ramp throats; frontage vehicles must yield.

TTC § 545.157 • MOVE OVER / SLOW DOWN ACT EMERGENCY CORRIDOR

When approaching a stationary emergency vehicle, tow truck, TxDOT maintenance vehicle, or utility vehicle displaying flashing lights on the roadside, an operator must: (1) Vacate the lane closest to the emergency vehicle on a highway with two or more lanes in the same direction, OR (2) Slow to a speed 20 MPH below the posted limit (or to 5 MPH if the posted speed limit is under 25 MPH). Violations carry fines up to $1,250.

FLASHING RED STOP MANDATE TTC § 544.008(a)

Flashing Red Has the Exact Legal Force of a Stop Sign

Under Texas Transportation Code § 544.008(a), an illuminated flashing red signal requires the operator to bring the vehicle to a complete stop at the stop line or crosswalk before entering the intersection. After stopping, the driver may proceed only when the right-of-way is clear and approaching vehicles have yielded. Rolling through a flashing red light is cited as running a solid red signal or stop sign.

alt_route SYSTEMIC TRAFFIC FLOW // DYNAMIC BUFFER ARCHITECTURE
TTC § 545.062

Highway vehicular platoons function mechanically like compressible fluid columns in thermodynamics. When vehicles travel closely packed at high velocities, any minor deceleration input by a lead driver triggers a backward-propagating compression shockwave. In traffic engineering, this phenomenon generates “phantom traffic jams”—abrupt stationary pileups that travel miles upstream without any physical roadway obstruction.

Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.062 (Following Distance), motorists are legally bound to maintain an assured clear distance between vehicles. Operating closer than statutory standards eliminates driver perception-reaction margins ($t_{pr} \approx 1.5\text{ s}$), ensuring that minor brake taps escalate into multi-car chain-reaction collisions.

1. FORWARD BUFFER

Maintain a minimum 2-second interval at ≤30 MPH; expand to 4 seconds at highway speeds (>30 MPH) and 6–8 seconds in rain or fog.

2. LATERAL ESCAPE

Avoid lingering in adjacent vehicles' blind spots. Modulate throttle to ensure an open lane or shoulder exists on at least one side.

3. REAR MARGIN

When stopping behind traffic, leave enough pavement visible beneath the lead car's rear tires to maneuver around them if stalled.

signpost THE 100-FOOT ADVANCE WARNING THRESHOLD (TTC § 545.104)

At 45 MPH, a motor vehicle travels 66.0 feet per second. Activating a turn signal 100 feet in advance provides trailing motorists exactly 1.51 seconds of notice—precisely equal to the human perception-reaction time baseline ($t_{pr} = 1.5\text{ s}$). Drivers who fail to signal until they begin braking force trailing drivers into panic braking, provoking rear-end impacts and lane-splitting hazards.

UNCONTROLLED 4-WAY RULE TTC § 545.151(a)

Simultaneous Arrival Mandates Yielding to the Vehicle on the Right

At an intersection not controlled by traffic signs or signals, the driver of a vehicle on the left shall yield the right-of-way to the vehicle on the immediate right if both arrive simultaneously. If one vehicle has already safely entered the intersection prior to your arrival, you must yield to permit them to clear. Never accelerate into an uncontrolled blind intersection expecting cross-traffic to yield.

policy TEXAS STATUTORY CODEX // PRIORITY SCENARIOS
TTC CHAPTER 545
1. UNPAVED ROADWAY YIELD (TTC § 545.151(b)) PAVED WINS

An operator on an unpaved (gravel, dirt, or caliche) roadway intersecting a paved roadway shall stop and yield the right-of-way to any vehicle on the paved roadway. Paved roadways carry higher design speeds and traffic volumes; entering operators cannot assert simultaneous arrival priority over paved traffic.

2. T-INTERSECTION TERMINATING STEM (TTC § 545.152(b)) STEM MUST YIELD

An operator on a roadway that terminates at an intersection with another roadway (the bottom stem of a “T”) shall stop and yield the right-of-way to any vehicle on the intersecting through highway. Drivers on through streets possess continuous legal priority.

3. PRIVATE DRIVEWAYS & ALLEYS (TTC § 545.155) FULL STOP REQUIRED

An operator emerging from an alley, private road, driveway, or building must stop prior to crossing the sidewalk line and yield the right-of-way to all pedestrians and approaching vehicular traffic on the public roadway.

4. PROHIBITED PARKING CLEARANCES (TTC § 545.302) SIGHTLINE BUFFERS

To preserve sight triangles at intersections, parking is strictly prohibited: within 15 feet of a fire hydrant; within 20 feet of a crosswalk at an intersection; within 30 feet of a stop sign, yield sign, or flashing traffic signal; and within 50 feet of the nearest railroad rail.
