Vehicle Roadworthiness & State Inspection

Safety Equipment Standards, Two Steps One Sticker & Two-Plate Mandate

Safe motoring begins before turning the ignition key. Under the Texas Transportation Code, operating a motor vehicle on public roadways is conditioned upon strict mechanical roadworthiness and valid state registration. Highway safety depends on physical equipment integrity—from stopping tolerances and tire tread depth to unobstructed multi-angle visibility and legal dual license plate display.

verified_user Texas Statutory Standard (Two Steps, One Sticker):

Under Texas Transportation Code Chapters 502, 547, and 548, safety inspection and vehicle registration are electronically synchronized. A vehicle must pass the comprehensive 16-point safety inspection before registration can be issued. Texas is strictly a Two-Plate State: passenger cars must mount plates on both front and rear exterior bumpers.

Front & Rear Exterior Mounting Mandate

Texas is a strict two-plate state for passenger cars. Both plates must be securely attached to the exterior bumpers. Displaying the front plate on the dashboard or inside the windshield is illegal and carries a Class C misdemeanor fine up to $200.

REGISTRATION PROTOCOL

Two Steps, One Sticker Workflow

The Texas Department of Motor Vehicles (TxDMV) and Department of Public Safety (DPS) integrated inspection and registration into a single digital process:

1
Step 1: Pass State Safety Inspection

Take the vehicle to any DPS-certified inspection station. The technician conducts the 16-point equipment test. Upon passing, the station electronically transmits the Vehicle Inspection Report (VIR) to the state database in real time.

2
Step 2: Renew Vehicle Registration

Renew online via TxDMV or in person at your County Tax Assessor-Collector. The state verification system instantly verifies your active electronic VIR and valid liability insurance (via TexasSure), then issues a single registration decal.

3
Affix Decal to Lower-Left Windshield

Under TTC § 502.059, the registration insignia must be affixed strictly to the inside lower-left corner of the front windshield (viewed from the driver's seat). Displaying expired stickers or misplacing the decal is a traffic violation.

warning New Resident Statutory Timeframe Divergence:

While Texas law provides a 90-day grace period to transfer an out-of-state driver's license (TTC § 521.029), vehicle registration must be completed within 30 days of establishing residency or employment (TTC § 502.040). Driving after Day 30 without Texas vehicle registration is unlawful, even with a valid out-of-state driver license!
