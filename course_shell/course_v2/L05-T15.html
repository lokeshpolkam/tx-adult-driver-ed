STATUTE AUDIT
TTC § 545.351 • THE BASIC SPEED RULE STRICT LIABILITY

(a) An operator may not drive at a speed greater than is reasonable and prudent under the conditions and having regard for actual and potential hazards then existing.
(b) An operator: (1) may not drive a vehicle at a speed greater than is reasonable and prudent under the conditions; and (2) shall control the speed of the vehicle as necessary to avoid colliding with another person or vehicle.
(c) An operator shall, consistent with Subsections (a) and (b), drive at an appropriate reduced speed when: (1) approaching and crossing an intersection or railroad crossing; (2) approaching and going around a curve; (3) approaching a hill crest; (4) traveling on a narrow or winding roadway; and (5) a special hazard exists with respect to traffic, including pedestrians, weather, or highway conditions.

• Legal Doctrine: Prima Facie Limits Nullified on Ice
Driving the posted speed limit (e.g., 70 MPH or 75 MPH) during freezing rain, sleet, or black ice conditions is an unlawful moving violation. Texas peace officers issue citations under § 545.351 regardless of the speed signs posted along the corridor.
TTC § 547.302 • HEADLAMP & ILLUMINATION MANDATE SAFETY CODE

Under Texas law, drivers must display illuminated headlamps whenever visibility is unfavorable or atmospheric conditions (such as winter precipitation, sleet, snow, or dense fog) reduce sight distance below 1,000 feet. Drivers must use low-beam headlights in winter precipitation because high-beam headlights reflect blinding glare off airborne ice crystals and falling snow back into the driver's eyes.

Why Elevated Flyovers and Bridges Freeze Hours Before Roadbeds

Standard highway roadbeds rest directly upon deep earth soil, which acts as a vast geothermal heat sink storing latent warmth from previous daylight. By contrast, elevated bridge decks, highway overpasses, and direct-connector flyovers are suspended in mid-air. Freezing ambient air circulates simultaneously across the top pavement and beneath the concrete deck underside. This dual-surface convective heat loss causes the bridge structure to plummet below 32°F (0°C) and flash-freeze hours before surrounding ground highways show any ice.

NEWTONIAN CONVECTION

Thermal dissipation in civil highway infrastructure is governed by Newton's Law of Cooling, expressed by the convective heat transfer equation:

q = h × A × (Tsurface - T∞)

Where h represents the convective heat transfer coefficient (heavily magnified by high wind speeds across open river valleys and highway flyovers), A is the exposed surface area, Tsurface is the bridge deck temperature, and T∞ is ambient air temperature.

EARTH-GROUND ROADBED INSULATED
  • • Exposed Faces: Only 1 surface (top pavement).
  • • Subsurface Base: Semi-infinite geothermal soil mass.
  • • Thermal Conductivity: Earth acts as a massive thermal battery, storing calories absorbed during sunlight.
  • • Freezing Lag: Often remains 4°F to 8°F warmer than surrounding air for several hours.
SUSPENDED BRIDGE DECK SUSPENDED
  • • Exposed Faces: 2 major surfaces (deck top + undercarriage girder void).
  • • Thermal Isolation: Suspended in free air; zero thermal connection to earth bedrock.
  • • Wind Advection: Sub-freezing air velocity underneath accelerates convective cooling exponentially.
  • • Flash Freeze: Freezes instantly once air drops below 32°F, creating invisible black ice.
OPTICAL DANGER // TRANSPARENT BLACK ICE μ = 0.05 – 0.08

"Black ice" is not black; it is chemically pure, completely transparent ice that forms without trapped air bubbles. Because it is crystalline and perfectly clear, the black asphalt or gray concrete pavement underneath is clearly visible. To approaching drivers, the road appears merely wet and reflective. Motorists fail to recognize that the pavement is completely lubricated by ice until lateral tire friction evaporates.

Friction Plummets to μ = 0.08: Never Pump Anti-Lock Brakes

On black ice, the tire friction coefficient plummets from 0.80 to 0.08, expanding total stopping distance tenfold—from 180 feet to over 1,800 feet (six football fields). In modern vehicles equipped with four-wheel Anti-lock Braking Systems (ABS), the driver must apply continuous, maximum pedal pressure: "Stomp, Stay, and Steer." Pumping the brake pedal dumps hydraulic pressure, resets computer sensor cycles, and severely increases stopping distance. Never activate cruise control on winter roads.

motion_sensor_active BRAKING DYNAMICS // MODERN ABS OPERATION
15-20 HZ PULSING

Prior to the widespread adoption of modern electronic brake control modules, driver education curricula taught motorists to manually pump mechanical brake pedals during skids. In any modern motor vehicle equipped with Anti-lock Brakes (ABS), manually pumping the brake pedal is a catastrophic driving error.

1. STOMP

Depress the brake pedal immediately with maximum force. Do not ease onto the pedal or hesitate.

2. STAY

Maintain solid, continuous, unyielding foot pressure. Ignore shuddering, grinding noises, and pedal vibrations.

3. STEER

Because ABS prevents wheel lockup, directional steering authority is preserved. Steer smoothly around hazards.

cancel CRITICAL PROHIBITION: NEVER ENGAGE CRUISE CONTROL

Activating cruise control on icy, snowy, or wet roads is extremely hazardous. When driving wheels lose traction on a patch of black ice, cruise control software interprets the sudden loss of vehicle forward velocity as a hill or headwind, instantly opening the throttle to accelerate. This sudden injection of torque spins the drive wheels freely, triggering an instantaneous, violent spinout from which recovery is mathematically impossible.

TEXAS HANDBOOK CH. 5
01 • REDUCED TRACTION ON PACKED SNOW/ICE

Countermeasure: Drastically expand following headway from standard 3 seconds to at least 8 to 10 seconds. Snow tires will slide on ice or hard-packed snow; never assume specialized rubber grants immunity to sliding.

02 • UNPREDICTABLE ROAD CONDITIONS

Countermeasure: Road conditions vary block by block or mile by mile depending on tree shade, solar exposure, wind corridors, and pavement grading. Reduce speed continuously to match existing friction.

03 • OBSCURED GLASS VISIBILITY

Countermeasure: Clear all frost, snow, and ice from every window, mirror, and headlamp lens before moving. Peep-hole driving through a tiny scraped patch is an unlawful moving violation.

04 • SHADED ASPHALT & ELEVATED BRIDGES

Countermeasure: Scan several blocks ahead for highway overpasses, bridges, tree-canopied roads, and northern building shadows where ice lingers long after other roads have thawed.

05 • UNKNOWN SURFACE ADHESION

Countermeasure: Perform a gentle traction audit test when first starting out: on a flat, clear stretch with no surrounding traffic, apply brakes gently at low speed to physically feel tire grip before reaching turns.

06 • EQUIPMENT LIMITATIONS

Countermeasure: While tire chains provide the highest mechanical bite in deep snow, Texas statutes note that neither tire chains nor studded snow tires permit traveling at normal highway speeds on slick surfaces.
