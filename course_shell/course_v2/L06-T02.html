TPC § 49.01(2) • CLINICAL INTOXICATION DEFINITION ALL SUBSTANCES

Under Texas Penal Code § 49.01(2)(A), intoxication is defined as “not having the normal use of mental or physical faculties by reason of the introduction of alcohol, a controlled substance, a drug, a dangerous drug, a combination of two or more of those substances, or any other substance into the body.” This behavioral standard operates completely independent of chemical BAC limits: any driver whose divided attention or coordination is compromised is guilty of DWI.

• Dual-Track Legal Mechanism: Number vs. Clinical Impairment
While 0.08 BAC establishes strict per se guilt under Prong 2 (§ 49.01(2)(B)), Prong 1 empowers Texas peace officers to arrest and prosecutors to convict motorists operating with zero alcohol in their system who are impaired by prescription medications, over-the-counter sleep aids, or cannabis.
TPC § 49.10 • NO PRESCRIPTION DEFENSE ABSOLUTE LIABILITY

Texas Penal Code § 49.10 explicitly establishes: “The fact that the defendant is or has been entitled to use the alcohol, controlled substance, drug, dangerous drug, or other substance is not a defense.” Holding a valid physician prescription, taking medicine exactly as directed by a pharmacist label, or consuming an over-the-counter medication confers zero legal immunity against a DWI charge if the driver’s normal faculties are impaired.

TTC CHAPTER 521 • ADMINISTRATIVE & STATUTORY SANCTIONS MANDATORY SUSPENSION

TTC § 521.372 (Automatic Suspension; License Denial): A person's driver's license is automatically suspended on final conviction of any drug offense under the Texas Controlled Substances Act, Texas Dangerous Drug Act, or federal drug laws. The mandatory suspension is strictly 180 days from final conviction. If the person has no license, DPS must deny license issuance for 180 days.

• Administrative Due Process & ALR Notice (TTC § 521.295):
Under TTC § 521.295, DPS provides written notice specifying suspension grounds and dates. The driver has 15 days to request an Administrative License Revocation (ALR) hearing to contest the action.

Biological Elimination • Zero-Order Kinetics

The human liver eliminates alcohol through enzymatic oxidation (alcohol dehydrogenase) at an unalterable biological rate of approximately 0.015 g/dL per hour (roughly one standard drink per hour). Cold showers, black coffee, intense exercise, and energy drinks do NOT accelerate metabolic clearance. Coffee merely produces a “wide-awake drunk” whose reaction latency and tunnel vision remain lethally impaired. Only the passage of time cures intoxication.

Total stopping distance is mathematically governed by perception-reaction distance plus mechanical braking distance:

D_total = (v × 1.467 × t_pr) + (v² / (30 × f))

Driver's License Reinstatement Requirements & Sanctions

Suspension under Texas Transportation Code Chapter 521 does not lift automatically after 180 days. Reinstatement requires completing three statutory prerequisites:

1. DOEP COURSE

Complete a 15-hour TDLR-certified Drug Offender Education Program under TTC § 521.374. Failure to complete makes suspension indefinite.

2. REINSTATEMENT FEE

Remit statutory administrative reinstatement fees ($100 ALR fee under TTC § 521.313 and § 521.377) to the Department of Public Safety.

3. SR-22 INSURANCE

File and maintain an active SR-22 Certificate of Financial Responsibility with DPS for two continuous years as proof of required liability coverage.
