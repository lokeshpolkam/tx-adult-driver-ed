OPERATIONAL DICHOTOMY // STATUTORY MECHANISMS

Mandatory Revocations vs. Administrative DPS Authority

Texas law separates automatic judicial sanctions following court convictions from proactive administrative enforcement executed by DPS record monitors.

Automatic Judicial Mandate

Mandatory Revocations (TTC § 521.271)

gavel

Upon final judicial conviction in a court of law, the clerk immediately transmits the abstract of judgment to DPS, requiring automatic, non-discretionary license withdrawal.

NODE 01: IMPAIRED DRIVING PENAL CH 49
DWI, Intoxication Assault (causing serious bodily injury), or Intoxication Manslaughter. Mandatory 180-day to 2-year statutory license withdrawal.
NODE 02: SERIOUS HARM & FATALITIES TTC § 550.021
Failure to Stop and Render Aid (Hit & Run with injury/death), Vehicular Manslaughter, or Criminally Negligent Homicide committed with a motor vehicle.
NODE 03: IDENTITY & REGISTRATION FRAUD TTC § 521.451
Displaying or possessing an altered driver license, manufacturing counterfeit government credentials, or making false sworn statements to DPS examiners.

KNOWLEDGE SYNTHESIS // STATUTORY CONSEQUENCE TRACER

Checkpoint: Trace the Legal Consequence

Test your comprehension of administrative vs. criminal sanctions under Texas Implied Consent.

help STATUTORY SCENARIO PROMPT

A 22-year-old driver is lawfully stopped for weaving at 1:45 AM. The officer detects alcohol and requests a breath specimen. The driver outright refuses to blow into the intoxilyzer. What is the immediate administrative consequence under Texas Implied Consent law?

[ A ]
No immediate consequence until the criminal court issues a verdict on the DWI charge.
Incorrect. Administrative license revocation (ALR) is legally separate from criminal prosecution. Implied consent penalties trigger immediately upon refusal.
[ B ]
Immediate license confiscation, issuance of a 40-day temporary driving permit, and a mandatory 180-day ALR suspension under TTC Chapter 724.
Correct (TTC § 724.035). Refusal results in immediate seizure of the physical license, service of a Notice of Suspension acting as a 40-day permit, and a 180-day suspension (if no prior ALR contacts), completely separate from any criminal court acquittal.
[ C ]
Automatic 90-day license suspension, identical to failing the chemical test at 0.08 BAC.
Incorrect. 90 days is the penalty for adults who agree to test and fail (0.08+ BAC). Refusal carries a heavier 180-day penalty.
[ D ]
Mandatory lifetime revocation of all Texas commercial and non-commercial driving credentials.
Incorrect. A first refusal is a 180-day administrative suspension, not a permanent revocation.
