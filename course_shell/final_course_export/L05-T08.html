STATUTORY FOUNDATION: TTC § 545.351 & § 545.157

§ 545.351 (The Basic Speed Rule): An operator may not drive at a speed greater than is reasonable and prudent under the conditions and having regard for actual and potential hazards. Speed must be controlled to avoid collision.
§ 545.157 (Grade Crossings): An operator approaching a railroad crossing must stop not closer than 15 feet and not farther than 50 feet from the nearest rail under designated active signal, bell, or visual conditions.

Sub-Section 08-A

Atmospheric & Weather Adjustments

cloud
dark_mode

Reduced Illumination

At night, driving speeds must allow for safe stops within headlamp illumination range. Standard low beams project approximately 150–200 ft; high beams reach 350–400 ft. Never overdrive your headlamps.

foggy

Fog & Rain Mandate

Under TTC § 547.333, activate low-beam headlights during fog, heavy rain, or mist. High-beam headlights bounce directly back into the driver's retina via airborne water droplets (backscatter glare).

ac_unit

Thermal Anomalies

Elevated bridges and highway overpasses freeze first because cold ambient air circulates above and below the deck structure. Snow and black ice thaw significantly slower in tree-shaded and cut-through areas.

info

Hydroplaning Protocol: On wet pavement, tires can lose hydro-dynamic surface grip starting at speeds as low as 35 MPH. If water buildup separates tread from the road, ease off the accelerator smoothly; do not brake abruptly.

Sub-Section 08-B // TTC § 545.157

Railroad Grade Crossing Mandates

railway_alert

THE 15-TO-50 FOOT STATUTORY WINDOW

When mechanical flashing signals, descending gates, crossing flaggers, or audible train horns indicate an approaching train, the vehicle operator must bring the vehicle to a full stop between 15 feet and 50 feet from the nearest rail. Never creep forward or weave around downed gates.

warning EMERGENCY STALLED VEHICLE PROTOCOL

If your vehicle stalls on tracks or becomes trapped between crossing gates:

  1. Evacuate Immediately: Order all occupants out of the passenger compartment without pausing to collect personal property.
  2. Run at a 45-Degree Angle Toward the Train: Run alongside the railroad right-of-way toward the direction from which the train is approaching, while moving away from the tracks. Running away from the train places you in the direct projection cone of airborne debris upon impact.
  3. Locate the Emergency Notification System (ENS) Sign: Find the blue placard attached to the crossbuck post or control housing. Call the 1-800 emergency number and transmit the 6-digit crossing number with check letter to stop all train traffic on that corridor.
Sub-Section 08-C

In-Transit Mechanical Emergencies & Recovery

car_repair
eject

Total Brake Failure

Rapidly pump the brake pedal to build residual hydraulic fluid pressure. Shift transmission progressively to lower gears (D → 2 → 1 / L). Apply the emergency parking brake gradually while depressing the release ratchet button to prevent rear-wheel lockup and secondary skidding.

tire_repair

High-Speed Tire Blowout

Do not apply hard brakes. Firmly grip the steering wheel at 9-and-3 o’clock to counteract violent lateral pull. Ease foot smoothly off the accelerator pedal. Allow vehicular momentum to decrease naturally before steering safely onto the right paved shoulder.

navigation

Skid Correction (Friction Loss)

Remove foot from accelerator pedal; avoid brake inputs entirely. Steer immediately in the direction the rear end of the vehicle is sliding (counter-steering). As straight-line tracking resumes, gently center the wheel to prevent secondary fishtailing.

hotel

Highway Hypnosis & Fatigue

Plan mandatory rest stops every 2 hours or 100 miles. Continuously shift visual scanning every 2–3 seconds across rearview, side mirrors, and far horizons. Never stare fixatedly at lane lines or the preceding vehicle's taillights.

SPECIALIZED ADVERSE CONDITION // TTC § 545.0541

Flash Floods & Recreational Water Safety

TURN AROUND DON'T DROWN
flood
TEXAS FLASH FLOOD REALITY // #1 WEATHER KILLER

Flash flooding is the number one weather-related cause of death in Texas. Approximately 75% to 76% of all flood fatalities in Texas occur inside motor vehicles, predominantly when drivers attempt to navigate low-water crossings at night.

6 INCHES OF WATER
Chassis Contact

Reaches the undercarriage of most passenger cars, causing loss of directional control and stalling. Swift current easily knocks an adult pedestrian off their feet.

12 INCHES (1 FOOT)
Passenger Car Buoyancy

Exerts sufficient upward buoyant force to float and sweep away most passenger cars. Tire friction drops to zero, rendering brakes and steering completely useless.

24 INCHES (2 FEET)
SUV & Truck Swept Away

Sweeps away heavy pickup trucks, four-wheel-drive SUVs, and commercial vans. Murky water conceals washed-out roadbeds, missing culverts, and downed electrical lines.

gavel Legal Penalties for Disobeying Flood Barricades
• Driving Around Barricades: Under Texas Transportation Code, driving around or removing a flood barrier at a low-water crossing is a Class B Misdemeanor punishable by a fine up to $2,000 and up to 180 days in county jail.
• Rescue Cost Recovery: Under Texas statute, local municipalities and emergency service districts are legally authorized to bill reckless drivers for the full expense of emergency swift-water rescue operations.
