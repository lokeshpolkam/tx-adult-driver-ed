Speed Related to Stopping: Roadway Conditions

Physics dictating kinetic deceleration cannot be negotiated. Under Texas law, posted speed limits represent the maximum ceiling under ideal circumstances. When weather reduces friction or darkness constrains sightlines, driving at the posted limit constitutes an actionable traffic offense.

Statutory Standard
Strict Circumstantial Prudence
Texas Transp. Code § 545.351(a): "An operator may not drive at a speed greater than is reasonable and prudent under the circumstances then existing... An operator shall control the speed of the vehicle as necessary to avoid colliding with another person or vehicle."

Wet Weather Doctrine: Hydroplaning & Gap Cushion

TTC § 545.062

At speeds above 35 MPH, tire grooves fail to evacuate standing water faster than forward momentum compresses it. Tire sipes lose mechanical interlock with roadway aggregate, lifting the car onto a water film. Maintain a minimum 2-second gap for speeds 30 mph or less, and a 4-second gap for speeds above 30 mph. A 4-second following interval is best practice for beginning drivers. Under Texas law, wet roads demand an expanded 4-second following gap (even at low speeds).

Action: Gently ease off accelerator; never slam anti-lock brakes during a plane.

Winter Hazard: Bridges & Overpasses Freeze First

TxDOT Spec

Unlike contiguous soil-insulated roadways, elevated bridges and overpasses have freezing air circulating both above and beneath the concrete deck. Additionally, snow melts more slowly in shady areas, maintaining ice hazards long after the sun emerges. Moisture flashes into transparent black ice at 32°F while approaching roads appear damp. Braking distance triples or quadruples; locking brakes guarantees unrecoverable vehicle yaw and spin-out.

Rule: Cease acceleration before bridge deck entry; maintain straight steering.

§ 547.302 LIGHTING STANDARD

Texas statute mandates headlamp activation from 30 minutes after sunset until 30 minutes before sunrise, or whenever visibility drops below 1,000 feet.

THE 45 MPH LOW-BEAM CEILING

Standard low-beams illuminate approximately 160 feet. At 45 MPH, total stopping distance is ~164 feet. Traveling faster than 45 MPH on low-beams guarantees over-driving your vision.

LEGAL CULPABILITY

Striking a stationary unlit object at night carries an almost insurmountable presumption of driver negligence under the Basic Speed Rule (§ 545.351).

Knowledge Verification: Stopping Physics & Roadway Conditions

TDLR Competency Req.
Question 01 of 02 TTC § 545.351

Why does traveling at the posted 70 MPH speed limit during a heavy rainstorm constitute an unlawful traffic offense in Texas?

✓ CORRECT: Texas Transportation Code § 545.351 establishes strict liability for operating at speeds exceeding what ambient surface friction can safely support.
✕ INCORRECT: The Basic Speed Rule mandates prudent deceleration whenever hazardous surface or sight conditions exist, regardless of posted numbers.
Question 02 of 02 TTC § 547.302

If a vehicle's standard low-beam headlights illuminate the roadway up to approximately 160 feet ahead, what constitutes "over-driving your headlights"?

✓ CORRECT: Driving faster than 45 MPH on standard low-beams creates an optical deficit where obstacles first appear inside your non-negotiable braking zone.
✕ INCORRECT: Over-driving headlights specifically refers to kinetic stopping distance exceeding forward optical sightlines.
