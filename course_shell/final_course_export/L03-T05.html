Passing Stopped Emergency Vehicles

Texas statute codifies strict kinetic safeguards when emergency and roadside service vehicles occupy highway shoulders. Drivers must vacate the adjacent lane or decelerate substantially under the Move Over / Slow Down law.

policy
TEXAS STATUTE MANDATE — TTC § 545.157

"On a highway with two or more lanes in the same direction, vacate the lane closest to the stopped emergency vehicle, or slow down 20 mph below the speed limit (or to 5 mph if the limit is under 25 mph)."

SECTION 02 // LEGAL DEFINITIONS

Which Vehicles Are Covered?

Texas law protects more than just police cars. The Move Over / Slow Down statute applies to any authorized vehicle with active flashing lights.

1

Following Prohibited

An operator may not follow an authorized emergency vehicle answering an alarm closer than 500 feet, or drive into or park closer to where emergency gear is operating.

2

Incident Block Ban

A driver may not drive into or park a vehicle within the block where a fire apparatus has stopped to answer an alarm, unless directed by a police officer.

§

Statutory Penalty

Class C Misdemeanor: $200 to $500 fine. Subsequent violations or offenses resulting in bodily injury trigger enhanced misdemeanor or felony tiers.
