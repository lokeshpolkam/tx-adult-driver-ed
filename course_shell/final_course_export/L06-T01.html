Legal Definition of Intoxication in Texas

Texas statute establishes strict objective chemical thresholds alongside clinical standards of behavioral impairment. Understanding these codified boundaries is required under Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) curricula.

Statutory Precept // Texas Penal Code § 49.01(2) State Codified Standard

"Intoxicated" means: (A) not having the normal use of mental or physical faculties by reason of the introduction of alcohol, a controlled substance, a drug, a dangerous drug, a combination of two or more of those substances, or any other substance into the body; OR (B) having an alcohol concentration of 0.08 or more.

Two-Pronged Legal Definition of Intoxication

Under Texas law, an adult operator commits the criminal offense of Driving While Intoxicated (DWI) if either condition occurs:

  • [ 01 ]
    Chemical Per Se Limit: A Blood Alcohol Concentration (BAC) of 0.08% or higher, regardless of outward symptoms or driving performance.
  • [ 02 ]
    Faculties Impairment: Loss of the normal use of mental or physical faculties due to alcohol, drugs, or a combination—even if the BAC is below 0.08%.
Standard Offense: Class B Misdemeanor Min. 72 hrs confinement
Minor Standard (Under 21) ATC § 106.041

The Texas "Zero Tolerance" Mandate

Texas enforces absolute Zero Tolerance for underage drivers. A minor commits the criminal offense of Driving Under the Influence (DUI) by Minor under strict statutory terms:

  • [ 01 ]
    Any Detectable Amount: Operating a motor vehicle in a public place with ANY detectable trace of alcohol in the minor's system (even 0.01% BAC).
  • [ 02 ]
    Zero Protection: The 0.08% standard does not apply to protect minors from prosecution, mandatory license suspensions, community service, or alcohol awareness classes.
Offense: Class C Misdemeanor (DUI) Mandatory ALR 60-day suspension
