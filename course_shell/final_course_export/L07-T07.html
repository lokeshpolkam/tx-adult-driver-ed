The SB 30 Playbook

3 Operational Strata
Operational Sphere 01 // Constitutional & Statutory Safeguards
U.S. & TX CONSTITUTION
01 Vehicle Search Protections

You can deny consent to search your vehicle if there is no probable cause.

Under the Fourth Amendment, officers require a warrant, valid probable cause, or voluntary consent to conduct a full vehicle search. You have the explicit right to state: "Officer, I do not consent to a search."

CRITICAL RULE: Never physically resist or obstruct, even if you believe a search is unlawful.
02 Right to Remain Silent vs Identification

You can refuse to answer exploratory questions, but you MUST show ID.

Drivers have the right against self-incrimination regarding questions like "Where are you coming from?" or "Have you been drinking?" However, operating a motor vehicle carries the non-negotiable statutory requirement to display your driver's license.

STATUTORY ANCHOR: Fifth Amendment & Texas Penal Code § 38.02
03 Recording the Interaction

Drivers and passengers have the legal right to record audio and video.

Under the First Amendment, individuals are permitted to document roadside encounters on a smartphone or dashboard camera, provided recording devices do not physically impede, obstruct, or reach toward the officer.

LIMITATION: Keep phone hands within plain view; do not make sudden reaches.
04 Right to Officer Verification

Right to ask for identification, agency name, and verification.

Motorists have the right to politely request the peace officer's name, badge number, and division. If stopped by an unmarked vehicle in a dark corridor, motorists may slow down, activate hazards, and proceed to a well-lit public apron while dialing 9-1-1 to confirm the stop.

SECURITY DIRECTIVE: SB 30 Unmarked Vehicle Verification Protocol
Operational Sphere 02 // Mandatory Statutory Duties for Drivers
TEXAS CODIFIED MANDATES
01 Immediate Halt Mandate

You MUST immediately pull over to a safe location on the right shoulder.

Signal early, pull completely off active traffic lanes, shift into "Park", set the emergency parking brake, and turn off the ignition. At night, immediately turn on the interior dome light so cabin occupants are fully visible.

STATUTE: TTC § 545.421 (Failure to Stop is Class B Misdemeanor)
02 Display of Driver's License & Insurance

You MUST display your driver license and proof of financial responsibility.

Under Texas Transportation Code § 521.025, any person operating a vehicle must possess and display a valid driver license on demand of a peace officer. You must also produce proof of state-minimum liability insurance.

STATUTE: TTC § 521.025 & TTC § 601.053
03 Truthful Identity Disclosure

Giving a fictitious name, address, or fake ID is an arrestable criminal offense.

Under Texas Penal Code § 38.02 (Failure to Identify), giving a false name, fictitious residence, or deceptive date of birth while lawfully detained immediately escalates the citation into a Class B misdemeanor jail offense.

PENAL CODE: TPC § 38.02 // Strict Criminal Liability
04 Physical Posture & Remaining Inside

Never exit the vehicle unless commanded; keep hands anchored on the steering wheel.

Exiting the car unprompted triggers sudden high-risk defensive postures from officers and creates dangerous struck-by hazards on high-speed shoulders. Keep both hands visible at 10 and 2. Verbalize before reaching into the glovebox or console.

SAFETY DIRECTIVE: SB 30 Roadside Tactical Safety Standard
Operational Sphere 03 // Due Process & Dispute Resolution
ADMINISTRATIVE & JUDICIAL REVIEW
01 Roadside De-Escalation Axiom

Never argue the merits of a citation on the roadside.

The shoulder of a roadway is a dangerous kinetic environment, not a courtroom. Challenging statutory justification, arguing factual guilt, or losing composure escalates tension and significantly increases physical danger for both parties.

DOCTRINE: The courtroom is the statutory venue for dispute, not the highway.
02 Badge & Unit Identification Protocol

Note officer badge number, agency patch, and patrol unit ID.

If you feel an officer engaged in misconduct, acted unprofessionally, or exceeded statutory authority, calmly document: the officer's full name, badge number, police department/sheriff's office name, patrol car unit number, and exact location.

RECORD KEEPING: Document data immediately on citation receipt or notepad.
03 Formal Agency Complaint Filing

File a formal grievance with the employing agency's Internal Affairs.

Every Texas municipal department, county sheriff office, and the Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS) maintains a codified citizen complaint procedure. Complaints are investigated through official internal affairs oversight channels.

PROCEDURAL OUTLET: Formal written submission or online oversight portal.
04 Signing the Citation Mandate

Signing a citation is NOT an admission of guilt; refusal guarantees immediate arrest.

Under Texas Transportation Code § 543.005, signing a traffic citation only signifies a written promise to appear in municipal or justice court. Refusing to sign strips the officer of procedural discretion and requires physical custodial arrest.

STATUTE: TTC § 543.005 // Non-Admission of Guilt
