The Invisible Zone: Blind Spot Driving

Modern vehicle mirrors leave critical peripheral wedges unmonitored. On Texas high-speed corridors, occupying these blind zones deprives adjacent operators of situational awareness—converting ordinary lane transitions into catastrophic sideswipe collisions.

Texas Transportation Code § 545.060 // Driving on Roadway Laned for Traffic

"An operator on a roadway divided into two or more clearly marked lanes for traffic shall drive as nearly as practical entirely within a single lane and may not move from the lane unless that movement can be made safely."

Statutory Mandate: Safe lane departure requires prior situational awareness through active peripheral scanning, mirror verification, and head checks.

Rule 01 // The Mirror Rule

If You Can't See Their Face

If you cannot see the commercial truck driver's face in their exterior side-view mirror, the driver cannot see you. Never tailgate or cruise alongside an 18-wheeler.

TX Commercial Code No-Zone Mandate
Rule 02 // Physical Verification

The 90-Degree Chin-Check

Glancing at side mirrors alone fails the Texas DPS road exam. Texas law demands an active 90-degree chin-to-shoulder turn to physically inspect the B-pillar zone before any lateral movement.

DPS Examination Protocol BGE Mirror Calibration
Rule 03 // Backing & Turning

Never Cross Behind Backing Trucks

Under TTC § 545.415, backing operators must yield. However, large freight rigs possess a completely blinded 200-foot rear wedge. Pedestrians and vehicles must never step behind a reverse gear.

TTC § 545.415 Zero Rear Sightline
Formative Assessment // TDLR Checkpoint

Topic 5 Knowledge Verification

Pass Threshold: 100%

1. Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.060 and defensive driving doctrine, when is an operator required to perform a physical shoulder head-check?

Correct: Under Texas law, mirror checks must always be corroborated with a physical head check across the B-pillar before any lateral departure.

2. When driving near a large commercial truck, where is the most hazardous and expansive blind spot located?

Correct: A commercial truck's right-side No-Zone is exceptionally large, spanning across two lanes and requiring extra vigilance before passing on the right (which is discouraged).
