Litter Prevention, Illegal Dumping & Highway Debris Safety

Roadside trash in Texas is not merely an aesthetic eyesore—it is a serious criminal offense, a multimillion-dollar public expenditure, and a primary catalyst for catastrophic high-speed vehicle collisions. Learn Texas statutory penalties, cargo securing mandates, and defensive debris avoidance techniques.

STATUTORY PRECEPT // TEXAS HEALTH AND SAFETY CODE § 365.012
"A person commits an offense if the person dumps or permits the dumping of litter or other solid waste at a place that is not an approved solid waste site, including on or within 300 feet of a public highway, right-of-way, or other public property."

Vehicle Discard Violations: Discarding even a single cigarette butt, beverage can, fast-food wrapper, paper napkin, or plastic bag from a moving motor vehicle onto any Texas roadway or shoulder constitutes an arrestable criminal violation of this statute. Furthermore, allowing trash or unsecured materials to blow out of an open truck bed constitutes unlawful dumping under state law.

Road Debris as a Fatal Highway Hazard

National Collision Frequency

According to the AAA Foundation for Traffic Safety, vehicle debris causes over 25,000 crashes each year in the U.S., resulting in approximately 500 fatalities and nearly 10,000 severe injuries.

emergency Panicked Swerving

The Sudden-Steering Trap

Over two-thirds of debris-related fatalities occur when drivers violently yank the steering wheel at highway speeds to evade small debris, provoking high-speed rollovers or head-on crashes into oncoming traffic.

front_loader TTC Chapter 725

Mandatory Load Securing

Under Texas Transportation Code § 725.001, every open bed, utility trailer, or flatbed must secure loose materials with tarps, tie-downs, or nets. Failure to secure cargo carries heavy fines.

shield Defensive Debris Protocol for Texas Motorists:
  • 12–15 Second Visual Lead: Scan high and far down the roadway to spot tire carcasses ("road gators"), loose cardboard, and lumber well before reaching them.
  • Hold the Line on Small Debris: If an unavoidable light cardboard box or paper bag enters your lane, brake steadily in a straight line—never execute a violent evasive swerve into an occupied adjacent lane.
  • 4-Second Truck Buffer: Double your following distance to a minimum of 4 seconds when trailing landscaping rigs, gravel haulers, or open pickup beds carrying tools or furniture.
PUBLIC CIVIC INITIATIVES // TxDOT PROGRAM

The "Don't Mess with Texas" Campaign & Hotlines

EST. 1986

Created in 1986 by the Texas Department of Transportation (TxDOT), the iconic "Don't Mess with Texas" campaign was conceived specifically to reduce highway litter through public education and civic accountability. Texas was also the birthplace of the nation's Adopt-a-Highway program in 1985, which allows volunteer groups to adopt 2-mile sections of state roadway for regular seasonal cleanups.

call TxDOT Toll-Free Litter Reporting Hotline:

Citizens can also submit vehicle littering reports online at dontmesswithtexas.org. When you report a license plate, location, and time, TxDOT mails the registered vehicle owner a formal warning notice, educational materials, and a complimentary in-car litter bag.

Texas Roadside Litter Reality

TxDOT spends tens of millions of taxpayer dollars annually collecting over 360 million pieces of visible litter along state highways. Every dollar spent on trash pickup is a dollar diverted away from roadway resurfacing and bridge maintenance.
