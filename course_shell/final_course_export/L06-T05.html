Texas Open Container Law

Possession of an open alcoholic beverage container inside any passenger compartment is an absolute Class C misdemeanor under Texas law. Clear spatial boundaries dictate lawful storage versus criminal liability on all public thoroughfares, regardless of active consumption or operational status.

gavel
TEXAS PENAL CODE § 49.031 // STATUTORY DEFINITION
"A person commits an offense if the person knowingly possesses an open container in a passenger area of a motor vehicle that is located on a public highway, regardless of whether the vehicle is being operated or is stopped or parked."

Statutory Doctrine & Application

Texas law explicitly rejects the defense that the container was not being actively consumed or that the engine was off. Placing an open container in the driver console cup holder constitutes immediate criminal possession inside the passenger area.

balance LEGAL FOUNDATION

The Law (Public Highway Rule)

It is illegal to knowingly possess an open container of alcohol in a motor vehicle's passenger area on a public highway. This statutory rule applies unconditionally:

  • check_circle Applies while operating, idling at a traffic light, or moving.
  • check_circle Applies even if the vehicle is parked on the shoulder, ramp, or state rest stop.
  • check_circle Driver or passenger possession triggers individual citation.
warning TEXAS PENAL CODE § 49.04(c)

The DWI Jail Enhancement

When an open container is found in the vehicle during an arrest for Driving While Intoxicated (DWI), state law imposes mandatory minimum sentencing elevation:

STANDARD 1ST OFFENSE DWI MIN. 72 CONSECUTIVE HOURS JAIL
WITH OPEN CONTAINER ENHANCEMENT MANDATORY 6 DAYS (144 HOURS)

*Judges cannot waive or probat the mandatory 6-day minimum confinement period under Texas Penal Code § 49.04(c).

inventory_2 STATUTORY CRITERIA

What Counts as an "Open Container"?

Under Texas law, the definition is broad and strictly construed. Any bottle, can, or receptacle containing any quantity of alcoholic beverage is legally "open" if:

A. The manufacturer seal has been broken or severed in any manner.
B. The original cap, cork, or twist-off top has been opened or recapped.
C. Any portion of the contents has been partially removed.
D. It is held in an open cup, tumbler, flask, or cup with a plastic lid and straw.
tune SPATIAL JURISDICTION

Prohibited vs. Permitted Areas

The "passenger area" encompasses the entire interior designed for seating or readily accessible to seated occupants.

PROHIBITED BY STATUTE
  • Seating surfaces, center consoles, door pockets, and floorboards.
  • Glove compartment: Explicitly classified as passenger area under § 49.031(a)(2), even if locked.
  • Unlocked center armrest compartments.
STATUTORILY PERMITTED
  • Locked trunk of the motor vehicle.
  • Area behind the upright last row of seats if vehicle has no separate trunk (hatchback/SUV).
  • Locked bed or tool storage compartment of a pickup truck.
