Human Trafficking Awareness

Under the Julia Wells Act (2021), Texas mandates human trafficking prevention education in all driver education courses. Learn to recognize warning signs at highway rest areas and transit stops, and know how to report suspected trafficking.

STATUTORY PRECEPT // Julia Wells Act (HB 2633, 87th Legislature)

“All driver education courses must include information on the recognition and prevention of human trafficking.”

Named after Julia Wells, a North Texas teenager survivor of sex trafficking. Texas Transportation and Education Codes mandate driver awareness to eliminate human trafficking across transit corridors.

Critical Warning Signs & Behavioral Red Flags TEXAS DPS & AG GUIDANCE
INDICATOR 01 // AUTONOMY DEPRIVATION

Deprivation of Personal Documents

The victim possesses no driver license, state ID, passport, cash, or credit cards. All identification and funds are strictly controlled by a handler.

INDICATOR 02 // PHYSICAL BRANDING

Ownership Tattoos & Physical Abuse

Visible branding tattoos (barcodes, crowns, currency symbols, or names) on the neck, chest, or wrists, accompanied by untreated bruises or restraint burns.

INDICATOR 03 // GEOGRAPHIC DRIFT

Transit Disorientation

The individual does not know their current city or state, has no personal luggage, or is transported rapidly between multiple metropolitan centers.

INDICATOR 04 // LABOR COERCION

Coerced Living & Work Isolation

The person lives and works at the same restricted location, works excessively long hours, and receives little or zero financial compensation.

INDICATOR 05 // EXTREME SUBMISSIVENESS

Coached Speech & Fear of Police

Answers are mechanical, rehearsed, or deferred entirely to a handler. The person displays extreme agitation or panic in the presence of law enforcement.

INDICATOR 06 // REPORTING DIRECTIVE

Life Safety & Hotline Activation

Never confront suspected traffickers. In immediate emergencies, call 911. For tips, call 1-888-373-7888 or report via iWatchTexas (1-844-643-2251).
