Procedures for Managing a Vehicle Breakdown

Sudden mechanical failures on high-speed thoroughfares create high-risk kinetic bottlenecks. Texas statute and driver safety doctrine mandate strict tactical steps to clear active travel lanes, evacuate occupants safely, and establish multi-channel visual warnings.

TEXAS TRANSPORTATION CODE § 545.301 // STOPPING, STANDING, OR PARKING

"An operator may not stop, park, or leave standing an attended or unattended vehicle on the main traveled part of a highway outside a business or residence district unless stopping off the highway is impracticable."

Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.301, steer completely off the main traveled way onto the right shoulder. Immediately activate 4-way hazard flashers. TTC CH. 545 & 547
01
TTC § 545.301 MANDATE • KINETIC CLEARANCE

Prioritize Safety: Move Off Road & Evacuate Safely

Under Texas Transportation Code § 545.301, stopping on the traveled portion of an active highway is strictly illegal whenever moving to the shoulder is practicable. If your vehicle stalls or experiences a blowout, maintain control and immediately glide as far onto the right-hand paved shoulder or dirt berm as possible.

MANDATORY EVACUATION PROTOCOL:
  • Exit via the Offside: All passengers must exit through doors facing away from active traffic (right-side passenger doors).
  • Stand Behind Barriers: Never stand or linger directly behind or in front of the disabled vehicle. Move behind the highway guardrail or up the embankment slope.
  • High-Speed Enclosed Traffic Rule: If barrier-free topography prevents safe pedestrian refuge, remain seated inside with seatbelts securely buckled to let the vehicle crash cage absorb secondary impacts.
02
TTC § 547.305 & § 547.501 • PHOTOMETRIC WARNING

Warning Lights: Activate Hazard Flashers & Interior Dome Lamps

Vehicles disabled along highway corridors are extreme hazards during twilight, inclement weather, and night driving. Texas law expressly authorizes vehicular hazard warning lamps (4-way flashers) to warn approaching drivers of traffic hazards and stopped vehicles.

1. HAZARD / EMERGENCY FLASHERS

Instantly engage your four-way hazard flashers. If emergency hazard lights are inoperable, keep parking lamps and taillights illuminated to broadcast rearward vehicle presence.

2. NIGHT INTERIOR DOME ILLUMINATION

Mandatory Night Rule: At night or during dense fog, switch on the vehicle’s interior ceiling dome light. This silhouettes the cabin, alerting passing motorists from far greater sightline horizons.

03
STATE POLICE & DPS PROTOCOL • UNIVERSAL DISTRESS CALL

Signal for Help: Tie White Cloth to Antenna & Raise the Hood

Texas Department of Public Safety (DPS) and Texas Highway Patrol officers look for standardized physical signifiers of distress. Do not wander across highway lanes searching for assistance. Use universal physical beacons to notify patrols and roadside assistance.

STANDARDIZED TEXAS ROADSIDE SIGNALS:
Raise the Vehicle Hood: Prop open the engine hood securely. A raised hood is an unambiguous sign recognizable at 500+ feet that mechanical assistance is required.
Tie a White Cloth or Handkerchief: Tie a white cloth, towel, or handkerchief to the radio antenna, driver-side mirror bracket, or left exterior door handle.
