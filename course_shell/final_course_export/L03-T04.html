Yielding to Emergency Vehicles, School Buses, and Pedestrians

Certain right-of-way protocols carry absolute criminal and statutory mandates under Texas law. Explore the three critical zero-discretion yielding scenarios below.

STATUTORY CODES: TTC § 545.156 TTC § 545.157 TTC § 545.066 TTC § 552.008 HRC § 121.007
policy
Texas Statutory Cornerstone // TTC § 545.157

Texas law establishes strict kinetic and spatial buffer zones around emergency personnel, student transit, and vulnerable road users. Failure to yield in these zones triggers mandatory fine escalations and criminal sanctions.

Statutory Decision Prompt

A law enforcement vehicle is stopped on the right shoulder with active flashing emergency beacons. What is your legal obligation under Texas Transportation Code § 545.157?

verified Both Options Are Statutorily Legitimate Under TTC § 545.157

The Texas "Move Over / Slow Down" law dictates a two-tiered statutory remedy: Driver MUST vacate the adjacent lane on roads with two or more lanes in the same direction. If lane departure cannot be executed safely, driver MUST slow to a speed 20 MPH below the posted limit (or to 5 MPH if posted under 25 MPH).

*Applies equally to: Police, Fire, EMS, TxDOT Highway Maintenance, and Licensed Tow Trucks.
Critical 500-Foot Apparatus Rule (TTC § 545.407)

An operator may not follow an active fire apparatus answering an alarm closer than 500 feet, or drive into/park within the block where the fire apparatus has stopped to answer an alarm.

Undivided Roadway: Mandatory Halting For All Directions
Class C Misdemeanor ($500 - $1,250 Fine)

On an undivided highway or street, traffic approaching from either direction must stop when the school bus displays alternating flashing red lights. You may not resume driving until the school bus moves, the red lights stop flashing, or the driver signals you to proceed.

DIVIDED HIGHWAY EXCEPTION

You are NOT required to stop if the school bus is on a different roadway separated by an intervening space or physical barrier (concrete barrier, grass median, unpaved strip ≥ 30 feet).

CENTER TURN LANE WARNING

A shared continuous two-way left-turn lane does NOT constitute a physical median! Traffic in both directions must halt completely.

*Repeat violation under Texas Transportation Code § 545.066 within 5 years triggers mandatory 180-day license suspension.

The Green Signal Paradox: Who Has Precedence?

Your traffic signal turns green, but a pedestrian is still halfway across the marked crosswalk in front of you. What action does Texas law mandate?

gavel Universal Duty of Due Care (TTC § 552.008)

Notwithstanding any other statutory provision, an operator of a motor vehicle shall exercise due care to avoid colliding with a pedestrian on any roadway, sound the horn when necessary, and observe proper precautions on observing any child or obviously confused or incapacitated person.

blind
White Cane Doctrine // Human Resources Code § 121.007

Motorists must take ALL precautions and come to a FULL COMPLETE STOP when approaching a pedestrian carrying a white cane (with or without a red tip) or accompanied by a service animal. Right-of-way surrender here is absolute, unconditional, and non-negotiable under Texas statutory law.
