Minor Penalties — Zero Tolerance

Under Texas Alcoholic Beverage Code § 106.041, the state enforces an absolute Zero Tolerance standard for drivers under the age of 21. Unlike adult statutory thresholds, an underage operator commits a crime with any detectable amount of alcohol in their system.

Statutory Mandate // Texas Alcoholic Beverage Code § 106.041 Strict Liability Standard
“A minor commits an offense if the minor operates a motor vehicle in a public place, or a watercraft, while having any detectable amount of alcohol in the minor’s system.”

Texas statute does not recognize a 0.08% BAC floor for minors. An odor of alcohol, field breath test reaction, or chemical trace triggers mandatory immediate judicial and administrative penalties.

Mandatory Enforcement Details

Under Texas Transportation and Alcoholic Beverage Code, operating with any detectable trace of alcohol immediately revokes the minor's driving privilege for 60 days. In addition to the fine and community service, the minor is mandated by statute to attend an approved TDLR Alcohol Awareness Course within 90 days.

Consequences of Repeat Minor Violations

Statutory Doctrine

Under 21? Texas law enforces strict ZERO TOLERANCE. Any detectable amount of alcohol while driving is a crime.

This applies regardless of whether the minor feels sober, operates safely, or registers below the adult 0.08% benchmark.
