Right-of-Way is Given, Not Taken.

Under the Texas Transportation Code (§ 545.151), the statute does not grant absolute authority to proceed through intersections at will. Rather, it imposes a statutory duty to yield to other vehicles and pedestrians. The universal legal duty of due care established in § 545.401 firmly establishes that preservation of life always supersedes mechanical legal priority.

Even when a circular green signal or green arrow is illuminated, entering an occupied conflict zone or failing to prevent a collision constitutes reckless driving under Texas jurisprudence.

gavel

The Golden Rule of Texas Roadways

Right-of-way is not property you can seize or claim by force. It is a courteous grant surrendered by others or conceded by prudent drivers to preserve human life.

SECTION 02 // JURISPRUDENCE & STATUTORY ACCOUNTABILITY

Legal Foundations & Penalties

Failure to yield is not a mere technical infraction under Texas law. When failure to yield causes injury or breaches critical safety corridors, statutory penalties escalate sharply.

General Right-of-Way Violations

Texas Transportation Code § 545.151(a) & § 542.401
Class C Enhanced / Civil Restitution

$500 to $2,000 Fine

Bodily Injury Collision
health_and_safety

Assessed when failing to yield causes a collision resulting in demonstrable bodily injury (bruising, concussions, soft tissue lacerations). Mandatory minimum court fine plus 2 Texas DPS points and automatic 60-day license suspension review.

Statutory Basis: TTC § 542.4045(a)
Severe Misdemeanor to Felony Escalation

$1,000 to $4,000 Fine

Serious Bodily Injury (SBI)
warning

Assessed when a failure-to-yield breach results in protracted impairment, bone fractures, or substantial risk of death under Texas Penal Code § 1.07. Triggers immediate 180-day license suspension, full civil tort liability, and potential state prosecutor felony indictment.

Statutory Basis: TTC § 542.4045(b) & TPC § 1.07

Protected Safety Corridors

Texas Transportation Code § 545.157 & § 545.066
Mandatory Stop Zone (15 to 50 Feet)

$50 to $200 Fine

Railroad Crossing Failure
train

Drivers who fail to halt between 15 and 50 feet when electric signals flash, crossing gates lower, or an approaching train sounds an audible blast within 1,500 feet. Repeat violations trigger enhanced criminal fines.

Statutory Basis: TTC § 545.157
Zero-Discretion Child Safety Corridor

Up to State Jail Felony

School Bus Visual Signal Passing
directions_bus

1st offense carries $500–$1,250 fine; 2nd offense within 5 years triggers mandatory driver license suspension for up to 6 months. If a driver illegally passes a stopped school bus displaying alternating flashing red lights and causes serious bodily injury, Texas law elevates the charge directly to a State Jail Felony (up to 2 years incarceration, $10,000 fine).

Statutory Basis: TTC § 545.066

The Defensive Driver's Affirmation

“The law provides rules; prudence provides survival.”

Never stake your personal physical safety on the expectation that another driver will obey legal commands. Concede the space, control your deceleration, and preserve human life.
